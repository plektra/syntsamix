# SSI2144 breadboard test plan

Goal: verify the simulation in `README.md` against the real chip, and fix the values the channel card schematic needs (output scale, resonance limit, drive setting). Record every measurement in the result tables below.

Order list: `breadboard-bom.csv`. Schematic, breadboard layout and placement tables: `breadboard.html` (generated by `breadboard_schematic.py`, `breadboard_layout.py` and `breadboard_page.py`; the layout script checks the breadboard connections against the schematic netlist).

Reference: SSI2144 datasheet Rev 3.0 (January 2018), Figures 1 and 3. Where this plan says "per Figure 1", copy the datasheet values exactly; they set the filter's frequency scale and are not repeated here, so they cannot be copied wrong.

## 2. Build

### Chip on adapter
Flux the pads, tack two opposite corner pins, check alignment, then solder the rest or drag-solder. Inspect for bridges under magnification and check pin-to-pin continuity with the multimeter before powering.

### Power
±15 V from the bench supply, current limit 50 mA. 100 nF ceramic from each supply pin to ground right at the chip and at each op amp; 10 µF per rail at the breadboard's supply entry. Note the supply current at power-up (datasheet: about 5 mA per rail typical; the whole board draws about 12-13 mA per rail with the pots). Two single-output supplies in series make the ±15 V: one's − and the other's + join as ground, their earth terminals stay open (a single supply's middle terminal is chassis earth, not a midpoint: with it, the board's ground floats and the rails read about +2.4 / −12.8 V).

### Bench notes (2026-10-15 to 10-17)
- Measure every kit resistor before fitting it: a "150k" read 135 kΩ, and the parts in the 33k compartment (banded 33k) read 19.2 kΩ (R6 is one of them).
- The DSO1060's meter reads resistance about 2 % low and cannot resolve capacitors below about 1 nF (100 pF reads 0.0 nF). In meter mode it reads only from its own COM and V/Ω jacks, not the probes.
- Trimmer pins and two-resistor pairs make poor breadboard contact: the gain wandered ±0.2 dB until R2 was fixed and RV3 replaced by a fixed 8.2 kΩ.
- Cutoff from one reading: with resonance 0 the four poles give f_c = f / √(1/√r − 1), r = V out / V in at a test frequency f above the cutoff. Read V in at the same frequency with the same meter, so the meter's own frequency response cancels.

## 3. Tests

Calibrate levels first: generate a 1 kHz sine from REW and set the interface output so the multimeter reads 1.228 V RMS (+4 dBu) at the breadboard input. Loop the interface output to its input with a cable and run a REW calibration so the interface's own response is removed.

The pot path is right (R12 296 kΩ, R13 100.8 kΩ): the knob's span falls short because the chip's tracking sags above about 5 kHz. The sag follows the frequency (control current), not the pin voltage, and is the same at ±12 V. The datasheet (Rev 3.0) gives no maximum cutoff or tracking figure; it claims 1000:1 for ±90 mV at ±12 V. Chip 1 also resonates weakly (test 2), so a second chip should be measured before this is taken as the part's behaviour.

### Test 2: Resonance onset and limit
With the 33 kΩ series resistor and cutoff at about 1 kHz, raise resonance slowly until the output sine-oscillates with no input. Record the wiper voltage and compute the current. Then change to 51 kΩ and confirm no oscillation at full resonance.

| Measurement | Datasheet / plan | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| Q current at oscillation onset | 350 to 450 µA (typ 400, datasheet p2) | Chip 1: no oscillation anywhere on RV2 (R6 read 19.2 kΩ: up to about 790 µA). Chip 2: none either, at any cutoff, on ±15 V and ±12 V |
| Max Q current with 51 kΩ | about 294 µA | |
| Oscillates at max with 51 kΩ? | No | |

Chip 1 (2026-10-17): with a small signal (0.12 V at the 1 kHz cutoff) the level at the cutoff peaks at a third of the input (k ≈ 1) at a wiper of 7.9 V (about 410 µA) and falls again over the last quarter turn; at 1.23 V in the peak is 0.396 V. Checked and cleared: pin 14 at 0.7 mV (the current flows into the chip), the R7/C8 junction at the same voltage (no DC through R7), C4 marked 561 at pins 4-5, C9 fitted, every capacitor-pin joint continuous from chip pin to adapter pin. Next: fit chip 2 on a fresh adapter and repeat this test first.

Chip 2 (2026-10-20), swapped into chip 1's place with the rest of the board unchanged (R6 still 19.2 kΩ, J1 off): no self-oscillation with the input quiet, with RV2's wiper at about 8 V (about 410 µA) or fully up (15.2 V, about 790 µA), anywhere across the cutoff pot's travel. The same on ±12 V (about 620 µA at the top). Pin 14 at about 10 mV with RV2 fully up, so the current reaches the chip. Supply current at ±12 V: +12.43 / −10.66 mA for the whole board, in line with the chip's datasheet 4.0 / 4.2 mA plus the TL072 and the pots. A `part-facts` check of the datasheet (Rev 3.0, Figures 1 and 3, p2) found the build matching: capacitors on the right pin pairs (560 pF at 4-5, 6.8 nF at 6-7, 10-11, 12-13), the optional 3.3 nF across pins 1-2 ("improves Q stability and amplitude over frequency sweep"), 470 Ω + 10 nF at pin 14 for 499 Ω + 10 nF, 200/220 Ω input terminations, pin 14 a current input at ground potential (0-1000 µA range, oscillation 350-450 µA), supply range ±4 to ±16 V with every figure at ±12 V. The only differences are the ±15 V supply (ruled out above) and the hotter input attenuator (18k / 200 Ω against the datasheet's 68.1k), which should not matter with the input quiet. Step 3 on chip 2 (±12 V, 0.12 V in at the 1 kHz cutoff): out/in 0.29 / 0.31 / 0.39 / 0.28 / 0.27 at wiper 2 / 4 / 6 / 8 / 10 V, k ≈ 0.6 / 0.8 / 1.4 / 0.5 / 0.3 from out/in = 1/(4 − k). Not reliable: with 0.5 V in, RV2 at 0 (wiper 0 V) and the cutoff fully open, chip 2 gave only 0.2 V (about 8 dB low) and drifted, while chip 1 in the same place gave a steady 0.380 V. Chip 2's adapter joints are suspect (inspect and reflow pins 1, 3, 8, 9, 15, 16 first); its no-oscillation result stands only once that is cleared. Chip 1's 0.380 V for 0.5 V in is about 2.4 dB under test 5's unity, possibly from the ±12 V supply; recheck at ±15 V.

Cause found (2026-10-20): back at ±15 V chip 1 wandered too, and tapping U1 sent the output from about 0.4 V to over 3 V. All 16 adapter pins read good to their strips and no bridges were found; wiggling the ladder film capacitors (C1-C3, placed diagonally for their 5 mm pitch) steadied the output at 0.44 V for 0.5 V in (cutoff open, about −1 dB). Poor breadboard contacts at the ladder capacitors are the likely cause of the drift and probably of the weak resonance on both chips (a series resistance at a ladder capacitor costs loop gain at the cutoff). Fix: solder C1-C4 and C9 directly across their adapter header pins (4-5, 6-7, 10-11, 12-13, 1-2), then repeat test 2 on chip 1 and chip 2's results after its joints are checked. Before soldering, after the wiggling: chip 1 still did not self-oscillate (input quiet, RV2 up to fully up, cutoff near 1 kHz). Step 3 on chip 1 (±15 V, 0.5 V in at 1 kHz, cutoff set for 0.125 V out at RV2 = 0): 0.154 / 0.175 / 0.193 / 0.204 / 0.189 V out at wiper 4 / 6 / 8 / 10 V / fully up (about 210 / 310 / 415 / 520 / 790 µA), k ≈ 0.75 / 1.1 / 1.4 / 1.55 / 1.35: a little stronger than before, still far from 4, and falling again at the top. Current measured directly with RV2 fully up: 14.9 V across R6, pin 14 at 10 mV, so about 776 µA flows into the chip. Chip 2 refitted on the steadier board: output 0 V or wandering with the cutoff fully open and RV2 at 0, where chip 1 gives a steady 0.44 V. Chip 2 is faulty or has a bad lead joint (it drew normal supply current at ±12 V); its test 2 results do not count. Reflow its leads with the capacitor soldering; the third chip is the spare. Chip 1 was then refitted one column to the right and powered: each pin sat in its neighbour's strip. Back in place, the + rail ran into the 50 mA limit; without a chip both rails read normal, so chip 1 is damaged (its results above, taken before, stand). Before powering a refitted adapter, check that pin 1 sits in its marked strip.

Where test 2 stands (2026-10-20): on a steady board, chip 1 reached k ≈ 1.5 at about 520 µA and never self-oscillated with 776 µA measured into pin 14; the build matches the datasheet. Not settled: whether the ladder capacitors' breadboard contacts still cost loop gain. Next: solder C1-C4 and C9 onto the adapter header pins, fit chip 3 (the last one in hand), repeat test 2, then the 100 Hz bass-loss check (output at 100 Hz with RV2 at 0, 10 V and fully up; k from the drop 1/(1 + k)). If chip 3 also stays near k ≈ 1.5, write to Sound Semiconductor with these figures.

Decide: if both chips need the same feedback within about ±2 % (about 20 cents over 3 octaves), an E24 pair can replace RV182 for the product (one part and one calibration step fewer per card). The warm-up drift tells whether the backlog item (decision 141) is worth bringing back.

### Test 13: Summer clamp, dropout and CV OUT (decision 165)
The card clamps the cutoff summer with two BZT52C11 back to back across its feedback, so the SSI2144 frequency pin stays inside its datasheet range (−120 to +150 mV) and below the low-cutoff dropout (above about +175 mV the filter goes silent and recovers at about +135 mV, datasheet p5). `simulation/cutoff/` predicts a clamp at about ±10.5 to 12 V and a pin within about ±128 mV; the knee below 0.5 mA is not in the zener datasheet. Electrokit has no 11 V zener, so the second order carries a pair each of BZX55C10V and BZX55C12V (DO-35): the two neighbours of the card's BZT52C11, and the two values this test may move to. Measure with both pairs; C11 behaves between them. Also needed, for the last row, a TL072 half with 100k in, 51k + 1k feedback and 1k out.

First without the zeners: feed the CV input from a lab supply (or a ±12 V divider) and raise the voltage on the low-cutoff side (positive at the pin) until the output goes silent; note the pin voltage, then lower it until the signal returns. Then fit the C10 pair and repeat across the full CV range, then the C12 pair.
