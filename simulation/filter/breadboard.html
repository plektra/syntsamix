Syntsamix · filter test circuit

SSI2144 breadboard

One SSI2144 ladder filter core with input attenuator, resonance and cutoff controls, the summer and the output stage, soldered on one Electrokit EKB14 board. Only the RV1 and RV2 pots, the three jacks and the bench supply are off the board, on flying leads; the RV3–RV5 trimmers are on it. It's the circuit for tests 1–7 and 11–13 in simulation/filter/BREADBOARD.md, built from both Electrokit orders, the SSOP adapter and an E12 1 % resistor kit.

Before you power up

  1. Check the adapter: confirm with a multimeter that DIP pin 1 connects to SSOP pin 1 (the chip's dot). Most adapters keep the numbering; a few don't.
  2. Solder the two socket strips first (rows D and G, columns 8-15), then the low parts, the upright resistors, the wires and last the flying leads. After soldering, check with the meter that no neighbouring strips are bridged, and that U1 pin 9's strip (15B) and U2 pins 3 and 5 (20G, 21D) read 0 Ω to the GND rail.
  3. Dot or notch to the left on both chips: U1's adapter with pin 1 at 8G and pin 16 at 8D (the adapter's pin rows are 500 mil apart, so rows E and F and the GND rail under it stay empty); U2 pin 1 at 18F. Before powering a refitted adapter, check that pin 1 sits at 8G.
  4. Power the board without U1 and U2 first: +15 V at 8A and 18A, −15 V at 15J and 21J, 0 V at 15B, 20G and 21D.
  5. With chips in, total current should be roughly 5 mA per rail for U1 plus a few mA for U2 (datasheet: SSI2144 about 5 mA typical). Much more means a short or a reversed chip: switch off.
  6. Pin 14 (Q) and the summer's inverting input (U2 pin 6) sit at about 0 V: both are virtual grounds.
  7. Pin 15 (cutoff) moves within about ±95 mV as RV1 turns (up to about ±127 mV with RV4 at an end; centre RV4 first). Volts there means a wiring fault. Above about +175 mV the chip goes silent until the pin drops back below about +135 mV (datasheet dropout; test 13): with a CV plugged in, a silent filter at the low end can be this, not a fault.

The pinout and the ladder, output and resonance values come from the SSI2144 datasheet (Rev 3.0, Figures 1 and 3). The input attenuator, drive jumper, resonance limit and cutoff scaling are this project's values, on the E12 kit's values and E24 values from the second order (the channel card's own where it has them: 18 kΩ, 200 Ω, 470 kΩ, 300 kΩ). A script checked the board layout against the schematic, connection by connection: no missing links, no shorts, nothing under a chip or a flat resistor.

Board layout

Electrokit ProtoPCB EKB14 (41003657). Columns 1–23, rows A–E on top and F–J at the bottom; each half-column of five holes is one connection, as on a breadboard. +15 V runs along the top edge, GND along the strip between the two halves (with two extra holes at each end), −15 V along the bottom edge. Resistors spanning one or two columns stand upright; R1, R4, R12 and R13 lie flat. The grey wires are insulated and may run over other parts. Off-board parts

Jacks (6.3 mm mono, PCB pins; Electrokit 41002006)
Solder short stranded leads to the tip and sleeve pins; leave the switch pin free. Tips go to the holes in the table above; sleeves to any free hole on the GND rail (the middle strip: columns 3-5, 24 and 25 are free). Use a TS cable from the interface (a TRS plug shorts its ring to sleeve, which is harmless). The CV jack is needed for tests 12 and 13.
Pots
Two full-size pots on flying leads: RV1 cutoff and RV2 resonance, 10 kΩ linear. RV3-RV5 are trimmers on the board. If the cutoff falls when you turn RV1 clockwise, swap its two ends. Linear RV2 crowds the resonance into the last part of the turn; that is fine for the tests.
Bench supply
+15 V, 0 V and −15 V to the three supply holes. Current limit about 50 mA per rail. Two single supplies in series: their earth terminals stay open (BREADBOARD.md, section 2).
