# Input module decisions

The passive input module (`hardware/input-module-6p3`) and its header interface (`docs/INPUT-MODULE.md`).

Full record (decision 154): the complete text of each decision as made, with figures, sources and rejected options. The current rulings, which sessions read first, are in `../input-module.md`; the index of all decisions is `../INDEX.md`. Numbers are global and never reused. Text marked **[proposed]** still needs the user's confirmation.

## Decisions

21. Mono input: L/MONO jack normalling (L alone feeds both sides); no panel mono switch
62. Input connectors live on a separate passive input module plugged into a header on the channel card (10 pins since decision 68) (interface in `INPUT-MODULE.md`). Mono normalling happens on the module. The prototype builds the 6.3 mm module; 3.5 mm and D-SUB bundle modules come later. Receiver, protection, AC coupling and trim stay on the channel card
67. The cutoff CV jack moves from the input module to the channel card's filter section, keeping filter controls together and and frees header capacity for the backlog direct output
68. Input header grows to 10 pins (1x10 Molex KK 254 style): pins 8 and 9 reserved for a stereo pre-fader direct output (backlog), pin 10 AGND. The cutoff CV jack sits on the top panel next to CUTOFF (item 106 moves it to its own spot above CUTOFF)
99. Input-module cable (system validation 2026-10-06, finding 7; refines item 68, changes `INPUT-MODULE.md`): both boards keep male KK 254 headers; they are linked by a 10-way 1:1 cable (pin 1 to pin 1) with a Molex 22-01-3107 crimp housing (KK 254, 2695 series, 10 positions, friction ramp) and 08-50-0114 crimps (22-30 AWG, tin) at each end, 26 or 28 AWG wire, the hot/cold pairs (pins 2-3, 5-6) twisted. Length set in the mechanical layout (about 10 to 15 cm expected). Part numbers from distributor listings; verify against the Molex drawings. A socket on the module plugging straight onto the card was rejected: it fixes the module position against the rear panel and rules out cabled D-SUB modules
105. Input header pinout confirmed as drawn (`/system` 2026-10-08; closes the pinout proposal in `INPUT-MODULE.md`): pin 1 AGND, 2 L+, 3 L−, 4 AGND, 5 R+, 6 R−, 7 AGND, 8 DO_L, 9 DO_R (reserved), 10 AGND. Both schematics already follow it; fixed before the channel card PCB. Rejected: a 12-pin header giving each direct output its own ground (new housing, both boards redrawn, for a backlog feature); leaving it proposed until layout (a late change would redraw two schematics and the cable)

179. Ready-made input cable (`/system` 2026-10-17; agreed by the user in the master session 2026-10-13, recorded here under the user's delegation to work the open items; refines items 99 and 160 and changes `docs/INPUT-MODULE.md`, Cable). The hand-crimped cable of item 99 (two Molex 22-01-3107 housings and 20 crimps 08-50-0114 per cable, about 80 crimps for the prototype) is replaced by the ready-made Molex 217796-1101 (SX-CONN-022, Mouser 538-217796-1101, $5.63, $4.65 at 25+, 2026-10-13): series 217796 KK 254-to-KK 254 off-the-shelf cable assembly, 2.54 mm single row, 10 circuits, female both ends, 150 mm. From Molex's page for the 300 mm sibling 217796-1102 (Conrad copy dated 2025-02-26; the -1101 drawing could not be reached from the sandbox on 2026-10-17): wire UL 11028, 22 AWG, insulation 1.57 mm max, crimped tin-plated brass terminals, friction lock; mates the KK 254 vertical header series 6410 among others (the card and module headers, footprint `Molex_KK-254_AE-6410-10A`, are series 6410); EU RoHS compliant per 2015/863 (Mouser: RoHS Compliant for -1101). Not verified for -1101: the pin-to-pin wiring (expected 1:1 for a dual-ended assembly, which item 105's pinout needs: a mirrored cable would put DO_R on L+), the housing part number and whether 150 mm is measured over or between the housings. So the wiring is checked on the Molex sales drawing (molex.com, part detail, Documents) before ordering, or pin to pin with a meter on delivery before the first cable is plugged in; the hand-crimp parts SX-CONN-009/010 stay registered as the fallback. Length: 150 mm instead of item 160's provisional 100 mm; the module stands on edge under the rear rail and the channel layout places its KK header near the rear edge, so 150 mm reaches with slack; the layout reports the header position. Wire: 22 AWG instead of 26-28 (the receptacles take it; nothing on the boards depends on the gauge). Hot/cold pairs (pins 2-3, 5-6) may be twisted by hand; inside the unit over 150 mm with balanced receivers the twist is a nicety, not a requirement. Cost: about €3.20 more per cable than the crimp parts, already counted in the estimate (decision 171, `docs/costs/offboard.csv`) and under cost watch (`docs/ROADMAP.md`, Cost-cut candidates item 8). Rejected: keeping hand crimping (too slow for the user, 2026-10-13)
180. Input cable as a self-made IDC ribbon (`/cost input-module-6p3` 2026-10-18, report `docs/reviews/2026-10-18-cost-input-module-6p3.md`; refines items 68, 99 and 179). The user: "Let's change to ribbon cables then" (2026-10-18), after the cost controller found the input module at €6.83 per board, 76 % of it the Molex 217796-1101 lead (€5.98 in `tools/costs.py`; Mouser 538-217796-1101 €4.28 at 10+, €4.00 at 25+, 2026-10-18). The ribbon on 2x6 headers: two Adam Tech BHR-12-VUA shrouded box headers (Mouser €0.445 at 10+, €0.354 at 100), two FCS-12-SG IDC sockets (€0.323 at 10+, €0.266 at 100) and about 0.5 ft of 3M 3365 ribbon (14-way stand-in, Mouser 517-3365/14FT €0.249 per ft; Mouser lists no 12-way), about €1.65 per channel at 10 pieces and €1.35 at 100. Saving about €4.3 per channel against the tool figure (about €3.8 against real Molex prices plus KK headers at €0.60), so about €17 on the 4-channel prototype and €69 at 16 channels; about €1.1-1.4 per channel and 20 crimps per cable against the hand-crimped fallback of item 99. Work: two presses per cable with the press the chain cables already need (decision 175); this reverses the 2026-10-13 choice of a cable with no hand termination, accepted by the user for the saving. Confirmed: the direction (IDC ribbon on box headers). **[proposed]**, to settle in a `/system` session before the channel card layout places the input header, together with `docs/INPUT-MODULE.md`, `docs/parts.csv`, `docs/costs/prices.csv` and both boards' footprints: the size 2x6 (2x5 is the Eurorack power size kept off the unit by decision 41; 2x4, 2x13 and 2x17 are the power, rear and audio ribbons; 2x7 is the alternative), the mapping of item 105's pinout onto 12 pins (spare pins as grounds), keying so a 2x4 power socket cannot seat in the 2x6 shroud (not checked), the Adam Tech shroud and key dimensions and RoHS statements for the Adam Tech and 3M parts (invariant 9; not checked), crosstalk of the ribbon against discrete wires (not checked; balanced lines, 150 mm), and the ribbon itself: 3M 3365 is only the priced stand-in, and decision 178 dropped the unrated 3365 for the chain cable, so `/system` picks a ribbon with a stated or verified rating (a signal ribbon carries little current). Headers without latches **[proposed]**: a latch holds the socket against tugging and vibration, but the 150 mm cable runs inside the closed case under no strain, and a latch adds about €1 per header (€8 at 4 channels, €32 at 16), about half the saving; hot glue or a tie holds a creeping socket. Also reviewed and rejected by the user (2026-10-18): cheaper 6.3 mm jacks. The Rean NYS216 (decision 132, €0.63) is used 2 per channel plus 11 on the master (19 / 27 / 43 jacks, €12 / €17 / €27 at 4 / 8 / 16 channels); LCSC HOOYA PJ-603 / PJ-611A class jacks at about $0.35-0.39 (2026-10-18) would save about €5 / €8 / €12, but state no mating-cycle rating (the 1,000-cycle floor of decision 135), mostly lack the threaded bushing the module needs to hang on its nuts (decision 160), do not all switch T, R and S (mono normalling, decision 21), and need new footprints, pin checks and possibly new rear-strip cut-outs; the user keeps the NYS216. Not taken up now (the user, 2026-10-18): one board for two channels' jacks (decision 160 stands) and an input module bundling several channels' inputs into a DB25 connector, which the user may add later; `docs/INPUT-MODULE.md` already keeps cabled modules such as a D-SUB bundle possible, so nothing changes in the design.
## Rationale notes

- Why L/MONO jacks: standard on pro mixers, no extra panel controls, nothing to forget on stage. Mono handling for AUX sits on the master card, so channel cards stay simple.
