# System rules

Project-wide rules that every board follows: jack sizes, RoHS, the shared jack part and the cost principle. Split from `system.md` (2026-10-10, file size); numbers unchanged.

Full record (decision 154): the complete text of each decision as made, with figures, sources and rejected options. The current rulings, which sessions read first, are in `../system-rules.md`; the index of all decisions is `../INDEX.md`. Numbers are global and never reused. Text marked **[proposed]** still needs the user's confirmation.

## Decisions

118. Jack size by function (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-09; replaces the "all jacks 6.3 mm, no 3.5 mm on the prototype" rule of item 27, which otherwise stands). 6.3 mm is the default for audio connections to other gear: the channel inputs on the prototype input module, the balanced main outputs, the AUX sends and returns, the headphones. 3.5 mm is allowed for Eurorack-level control connections (CV and gate inputs) and wherever 6.3 mm does not fit the mechanics. Each jack's size is recorded where the jack is specified (`SPEC.md`, `MECHANICAL.md`). First use: the channel card's cutoff CV input is a 3.5 mm Thonkiconn PJ398SM (body 9 mm, M6×0.5 bushing 5.5 mm with 4.5 mm threaded; a 1 mm printed washer on the unthreaded collar brings it to the panel underside at 10 mm and the nut clamps the panel), because no 6.3 mm jack fits the 10 mm card-to-panel gap of item 107 (a 6.3 mm plug is about 30 mm long; vertical PCB 6.3 mm jacks are about 24 mm deep). The master's EXT sidechain input (audio and Eurorack envelopes or gates) is chosen with the master section. Rejected: a narrow exception for the CV jack only (the next misfit would need another), no size rule at all (no consistency across the panel), and 3.5 mm wherever practical (unusual for balanced outputs and headphones, more wear on stage); for the CV jack also a panel-mounted 6.3 mm jack wired to the card (hand wiring on every card) and moving it back to the input module (reverses item 67)
122. RoHS as an invariant, standard passives, and the audit (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-09; refines item 116). (1) Invariant 9 in `ARCHITECTURE.md`: every component and every board complies with RoHS 2011/65/EU as amended by (EU) 2015/863, and boards are fabricated and assembled lead-free. (2) Standard passives (resistors, ceramic capacitors and standard diodes chosen by value and package, with no maker part number in `docs/parts.csv`) get their RoHS source when the BOM is fixed for ordering: the RoHS field of the LCSC/JLCPCB (or other) part actually picked, recorded in `parts.csv` then. Parts chosen by maker part number need the source when chosen, as item 116 says. (3) Audit before the first PCB order: a sub-agent goes through `parts.csv` on all boards, records a RoHS source for every part with a part number and lists those without one; the board sessions replace parts or obtain declarations, and the user decides the gaps. Rejected: a concrete part and RoHS source for every passive now (much work before the values are final) and no audit (older parts would only be caught at order time)
132. 6.3 mm jacks: Rean NYS216 (SX-CONN-015) replaces the Neutrik NMJ6HCD2 (SX-CONN-001) on every board (confirmed by the user 2026-10-09 as a cost cut, "change the Neutrik jacks to cheaper alternatives"; the part chosen by Claude, so confirmed (delegated); refines items 27 and 118, which stand). Rean is Neutrik's budget brand. The NYS216 is a 3-pole horizontal PCB jack, all contacts switched (T, R, S and their normals), with a glass-filled PA6 housing and an M11×1.25 plastic nut and washer (Rean drawing NYS216, 2015-05-27). Mouser 568-NYS216: $0.68 at 50 against $1.95 for the NMJ6HCD2 (both checked 2026-10-09), Active, RoHS compliant. That is about €1.15 less per jack, about €50 over the 45 jacks of a 16-channel build (32 on the input modules, 13 on the master). Trade-offs: rated for 1,000 mating cycles (Rean product page) against more than 10,000 for the Neutrik; plastic nut. Every jack sits on an FR4 panel and its sleeve goes to AGND through the PCB (item 61), so the plastic bushing changes nothing for grounding. Not a drop-in: rows 16.50 mm apart (NMJ6HCD2 16.23 mm), 1.5 mm holes, panel cut-out 11.4 mm, so a new footprint `syntsamix:Jack_6.35mm_Rean_NYS216_Horizontal`. The drawing does not name the pins: the footprint follows the NMJ6HCD2 arrangement (T R S row with S 4 mm behind the housing front, normals row opposite), which is inferred and must be checked with a meter on the first delivered jack before the input module and master PCBs are ordered. Whether the 1,000-cycle rating is enough for the much-patched inputs is checked on the prototype; the NYS215 (metal ferrule, $1.12 at 50) is the fallback (pin pattern not checked yet).
135. Cost principle: a live performance device, not studio equipment (confirmed by the user 2026-10-09). Every design and part choice weighs cost against what matters on stage. May be relaxed for cost: accuracy beyond what is audible on stage (channel-to-channel matching, meter accuracy within about ±1 dB, exact fader law, filter tracking accuracy); CMRR beyond stage needs and distortion below audibility; calibration (fewer trimmers, wider tolerances); studio niceties such as very low noise at quiet monitoring levels. Must stay: reliability and robustness (no failure mid-gig; connectors, panel parts, recovery after a power cut); headroom for hot Eurorack levels (±12 V peak, item 25) and the soft-clip behaviour; click-free mute and switching, no pops at power-up; noise and hum low enough for a loud PA; the ladder filter on every channel (the feature that sets the mixer apart; the user rejected it as a fitting option, 2026-10-09); safety and RoHS (invariant 9). Applied by every board session, by `/system`, and by the cost controller (item 136); a proposal that relaxes something names which side of this line it is on.
