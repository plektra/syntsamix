# Power decisions

The power board (`hardware/power`): 24 V DC brick input, protection, non-isolated converters to the raw ±20 V chain rails (decision 100). Chain voltage and power injection (decisions 40 and 80) are in `system.md`.

Full record (decision 154): the complete text of each decision as made, with figures, sources and rejected options. The current rulings, which sessions read first, are in `../power.md`; the index of all decisions is `../INDEX.md`. Numbers are global and never reused. Text marked **[proposed]** still needs the user's confirmation.

## Decisions

60. Prototype power: certified DC power brick (24 or 48 V) into the master section, isolated DC-DC module to about ±20 V, LC filter, per-card linear regulation. Power switch, resettable fuse, reverse-polarity protection, locking DC connector, power LED. A linear external supply box can be considered for a product version. (Item 100 replaces the isolated DC-DC module with non-isolated on-board converters and fixes the brick at 24 V; the brick provides the isolation)
81. The power input section is a separate small power board (`hardware/power`) next to the master card: DC brick input, fuse, reverse-polarity protection, power switch and LED, isolated DC-DC to ±20 V and LC filter. It feeds the master card and both power chains with short power ribbons, keeping switching noise and its ground currents off the audio boards. The master card takes power like a channel card and regulates locally. (Item 100: the conversion is non-isolated; the brick provides the isolation)
100. Power board conversion and parts (refines items 60 and 81; power board schematic session 2026-10-07). Isolated DC-DC modules were dropped as too expensive (about $260 for a ±20 V series pair of Traco TEP modules, the only family checked that trims down to 20 V). The certified brick provides the isolation; the power board converts without isolation and the brick's negative is PGND, so the brick's DC output must float (settled by item 101: invariant 8)
    - **Brick:** 24 V, Class II (2-pin C8 inlet) with a floating output: Mean Well GSM120B24-R7B (120 W) for the prototype, GSM160B24-R7B (160 W) for 16 cards (item 145: GSM220B24-R7B for 16 cards). Same plug and pinout: R7B locking 4-pin power DIN (Kycon KPPX-4P type), pins 1 and 4 = +24 V, 2 and 3 = 0 V. Mean Well GST bricks were rejected: Class I, and the 120 and 160 W models tie −V to earth
    - **Jack:** Kycon KPJX-4S, PCB right-angle at the board's rear edge, 7.5 A per pin; the KPPX plug latches into it. Check polarity with a meter on the first brick (Mean Well's and Kycon's face drawings are rotated relative to each other)
    - **Converters:** two TPS54560 (60 V, 6.3 A minimum switch limit): a buck to +20 V and an inverting buck-boost to −20 V (TI SLVA317), about 2 A per rail. Both synchronised at 400 kHz in opposite phase from one on-board oscillator (74HC14); the timing resistors give 400 kHz if the clock fails. Light-load pulse skipping cannot be turned off, but the cards' quiescent load keeps both in fixed-frequency operation
    - **Input protection:** 4 A resettable fuse (LUTE 2920L400/30GR), SMBJ24A TVS (clamps 38.9 V, keeping the inverter IC's VIN + 20 V under 60 V), two SQJ457EP P-MOSFETs back to back: one blocks a reversed brick, the other turns on slowly (about 7 ms) so the converters' input capacitors charge gently. The power switch only drives the shared gate. Power LED on the switched side
    - **Output:** one LC filter per rail, shared by the three headers: 2.2 µH (Bourns SRP7028A), 330 µF polymer (Panasonic EEHZK1V331P), 100 µF electrolytic for damping, 4.7 µF ceramic. Simulated about 47 dB at 400 kHz at the filter, 63 dB at the cards, peaking about 4.4 dB near 4 kHz. One 2 A resettable fuse (BHFUSE BSMD1812-200-33V) per rail per header, six in all; the chain 2 header is fitted on the prototype too
    - **Power ribbon parts (SX-CONN-008):** Würth 61200821621 box header, 3 A per contact; Würth 61200823021 IDC socket, 1 A per contact (two contacts per rail). Neither is stocked at LCSC: hand-soldered and ordered from a distributor
    - **Full size (about 4 A per rail; 2026-10-10: 3.31 / 2.69 A at the sizing figure, 4.37 / 3.68 A at worst case, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`):** the +20 V buck probably carries it (thermal check needed); the −20 V inverter does not (about 7.5 A inductor current) and needs a controller with external MOSFETs, none found at LCSC. Settled with the full-size supply after the prototype cards are measured (decision 96). The 2920 input fuse does not scale to 6.5 A either
    - Component values and their derivation: `hardware/power/scripts/design.py`. Confirmed by the user 2026-10-08: UVLO start 21.1 V and stop 18.7 V on both converters (they wait for the input ramp; on the inverter the stop threshold is moot once running); oscillator 4.7 kΩ / 560 pF (275 to 445 kHz over the 74HC14's threshold spread; both loops cross over near 1.8 kHz, the inverter's peak inductor current stays about 4.6 A at 275 kHz); power switch and LED on the power board at the rear edge (switch part to choose); part-number categories L (inductors, also ferrite beads) and F (fuses). Parts still to choose: the 100 µF 50 V input bulk capacitor and the 10 pF C0G 100 V sync capacitor
    - **Start-up ramp** (power board session 2026-10-08; settled by Claude, the user delegates such technical choices): with the fixed 2.6 ms soft-start both converters charge about 1.1 mF per rail at their current limit, and the averaged model `hardware/power/scripts/startup_sim.py` puts the brick's input at 183 to 262 W peak (6.3 to 8.8 A switch limit, SLVSBN0C), over the hiccup threshold of the GSM120B24 (105 % of 120 W = 126 W) and of the GSM160B24 (168 W) for 1 to 2 ms. Starting the inverter 10 ms after the buck still exceeds 126 W at the typical limit. Fix: on each converter 47 nF C0G (SX-C-016) from the output through a 1N4148W (SX-D-003) into FB; the loop holds FB at 0.8 V, so the output rises as an RC with τ = 240 kΩ × 47 nF = 11.3 ms (19 V after about 40 ms) and the input peaks at about 54 W whatever the current limit. A 1 MΩ (SX-R-060) and a second 1N4148W clamp reset the node; in steady state the diode is off and the loop is unchanged. On the inverter the network is referred to its IC ground (capacitor from PGND, clamp and resistor to −20V_CONV). The GSM120B24 stays the prototype brick
145. Full-size brick: Mean Well GSM220B24-R7B (`/system power brick` 2026-10-10; refines item 100, confirmed by the user after the ±15 V cost review `docs/reviews/2026-10-10-cost-system-15v-rails.md` and a brick survey). The 16-card console takes the GSM220B24-R7B (SX-MECH-005, 221 W) instead of the GSM160B24-R7B; the prototype keeps the GSM120B24-R7B (SX-MECH-003). Reason: at the rails 16 cards plus the master draw 120 W at the sizing figures and 161 W at worst case (`tools/system_power_budget.py`, 2026-10-10), about 133 / 179 W at the brick with about 90 % conversion **[estimate]**: 83 % of the GSM160B24's rating at the sizing figure and over it at worst case, 60 % / 81 % of the GSM220B24's, which leaves margin for a hot stage (working temperature −30 to +70 °C with a derating curve). One brick and one mains cord: the user rejected two bricks in series as unprofessional (2026-10-10). Facts from Mean Well GSM220B-SPEC (2026-05-09): IEC 320-C8 inlet, Class II, I/P-O/P 4 kVAC and 2×MOPP, no FG at the output; 24 V ±3 % (24.7 V maximum, as the GSM120B24, so the SMBJ24A note in the power board's `STATUS.md` is unchanged); 120 mVp-p ripple; 93.5 % efficiency, Level VI; overload 105-135 % in hiccup mode, far above the start-up ramp's 54 W. Same R7B plug (Kycon KPPX-4P equivalent, pins 1 and 4 = +V, 2 and 3 = −V), so the KPJX-4S jack and the power board are unchanged; 9.2 A rated, within the jack's 7.5 A per pin on two pins per pole. Price at TME (2026-10-10): EUR 72.90 at 1, 66.30 at 10, 64.40 at 20, 105 in stock; Mouser and Digi-Key about EUR 72 with no stock. RoHS: Mouser 709-GSM220B24-R7B RoHS Compliant (checked 2026-10-10). Item 100's full-size limits stand: the inverter and the input fuse still need the full-size supply design. Rejected: the GSM160B24-R7B (EUR 51.50 at Digi-Key; thin margin, worst case over its rating), the Mean Well OWA-200E-24 (200 W, locking XLR4 output at about the same price, but a fixed EU mains cord and 230 V input only), XP Power AHM180/AQM200 and Same Sky SDI200G (1.5 to 2.5 times the price, no gain), Class I versions (GSM160A/GSM220A: floating −V but the plug shell is earthed) and the GST220A24 (−V tied to earth, against invariant 8)

156. Power board converter dividers on fee-free E24 values (power board schematic session 2026-10-10, cost lever 5; refines item 100; confirmed by the user). UVLO divider R201/R301 732k → 680k (SX-R-036, LCSC C17797 preferred) and R202/R302 42k2 → 39k (SX-R-104, C25826 preferred): start 21.14 → 21.31 V, stop 18.65 → 19.00 V (SLVSBN0C eq. 4/5 with Ip 1.2 µA, Ih 3.4 µA, VEN 1.2 V); the inverter's EN at VIN + |VO| = 44.7 V is 2.42 V (internal clamp 5.8 V, abs max 8.4 V). The later start keeps the converters off until the input soft-start FET has finished its ramp, as item 100 intended; the buck's 19.0 V stop stays below the brick's 23.3 V minimum less the fuse and FET drops, and on the inverter the stop point is moot. RT R204/R304 243k → 240k (SX-R-084, the feedback divider's part, so no new type): free-running frequency 400 → 405 kHz, used only when the sync clock is absent. Buck compensation R203 15k8 → 16k (SX-R-005, C17490 preferred): calculated 15.94 kΩ (SLVSBN0C eq. 48), so 16k is closer than the 15k first proposed; Czero 220n and Cpole 560p stay (zero at 45 Hz, pole at 17.8 kHz). Four fee types fewer (732k, 42k2, 15k8, 243k; about $12 per order), none added. The 240k feedback resistor stays: it has no fee-free part, and moving the divider to 24k / 1k would need a 470 nF ramp capacitor, which is no longer practical in C0G (the start-up ramp τ = 240k × 47n). Rerun: `scripts/design.py`; `scripts/startup_sim.py` with the 21.31 V start (and the GSM220B24's 232 W limit from item 145): the 47 nF ramp still peaks at 54.2 W, rails at 19 V about 40 ms after switch-on. Netlist checks 0 problems, ERC 0/0.

157. Power switch: a snap-in rocker on the rear panel, wired to the power board (power board schematic session 2026-10-10; refines item 100, which placed a board-mounted switch at the rear edge; confirmed by the user, who asked for something cheap instead of a lever toggle). The switch only pulls the MOSFET gate to PGND through R102 (about 0.12 mA closed, 24.7 V open), so any latching contact rated above 25 V will do. Part: Legion SS11-BBIWG-R20-R (SX-SW-003, LCSC C309069, about EUR 0.32): black, O/− marking, 4.8 mm quick-connect tabs, panel cutout 19.2-19.8 × 12.9 mm by panel thickness (Legion drawing WIF120070 rev A4), 16 A 250 VAC, RoHS statement on the maker's drawing. It connects through a JST XH 2-pin header J102 (SX-CONN-016, B2B-XH-A, LCSC C158012, hand-soldered) with pre-crimped leads, so the rear panel can come off without desoldering; the leads are soldered or crimped (4.8 mm receptacles) to the rocker tabs. No datasheet of the cheap rockers states a DC rating, minimum load or contact material: check on the bench that it switches the gate reliably. The cutout and position on the rear panel go to `/system mechanical` (rear panel, still open). Rejected: E-Switch 100SP1T2B4M6QE toggle (B4 bushing unthreaded, so the board would take every flick; the threaded B3 version had no stock), C&K 7101MD9AQE toggle (threaded, about EUR 8.39: price), the channel cards' GPBS850L latching button (points up from a flat board, so it would move the switch to the top panel and is easy to press by accident on stage), cheaper KCD11 and RS601HL rockers (thin PCB pins not suited to wires, or no maker datasheet), E-Switch RA11131121 (cleanest datasheet but about EUR 0.55 at Mouser).

158. Power board parts left open by item 100 (power board schematic session 2026-10-11; confirmed by the user). Input bulk C103: Nichicon UHE1H101MPD, 100 µF 50 V radial 8 × 11.5 mm, 3.5 mm pitch (SX-C-028, LCSC C116229, about EUR 0.12), hand-soldered: JLCPCB has no fee-free SMD part at 100 µF 50 V, and the board already has hand-soldered radials. Nichicon CAT.8100M: 0.17 Ω max at 100 kHz (moderate ESR, good for damping the input filter), 555 mA rms at 105 °C / 100 kHz against about 0.3 A estimated, 7000 h at 105 °C, RoHS stated. Sync capacitors C207/C307: Samsung CL10C100JB8NNNC, 10 pF C0G 5 % 50 V 0603 (SX-C-029, LCSC C1634, JLCPCB basic) instead of a 100 V 0805 part (extended, $3 per order): C207 sees about 5 V, C307 bridges about 20-25 V (CLK_B from PGND to RT on the −20 V node) and about 40 V at most if the inverter output ran away (VIN + |VO| is limited to 60 V by the IC); C0G keeps its capacitance under DC bias. Rejected: KNSCHA 152EC0412 (cheaper, thinner documents), Panasonic EEU-FR1H101B (twice the price), an SMD bulk capacitor (fee); 100 V 0805 sync capacitors (fee, margin not needed). SX-C-021 and SX-C-022 (the placeholders) are not used.

159. Buck catch diode D201: MDD SS54 in SMA (LCSC C22452, JLCPCB basic) with the D_SMA footprint (power board schematic session 2026-10-11, cost lever 6; refines item 100; confirmed by the user). The drawn footprint was D_SMC while the recorded LCSC code C22452 is the SMA part: the footprint now matches the part, and the part stays fee-free. MDD datasheet Rev 2024A4: 40 V, 5 A, VF 0.55 V at 5 A, IR 50 mA max at 100 °C at rated voltage (about 4 mA typical at 62 % of rating, Fig. 2), RthJA 60 °C/W on 5 × 5 cm copper, TJ max 125 °C. Losses: forward about 0.15 W at 2 A (0.27 W at the 3.3 A full-size figure) at 17 % conduction; leakage at 24.7 V for 83 % of the time about 0.08 W typical, roughly 0.5 W at the datasheet maximum; rise about +15 °C typical, about +40 °C worst case, so near 90 °C junction in a 50 °C box with copper. Layout: give D201 a copper pad; bench check its temperature at full load. Rejected: an SMC SS54 (extended, $3 per order, likely the same die), SS56 60 V (lower leakage but VF 0.70 V).
