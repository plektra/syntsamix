# Power decisions

The power board (`hardware/power`): 24 V DC brick input, protection, non-isolated converters to the raw ±20 V chain rails (decision 100). Chain voltage and power injection (decisions 40 and 80) are in `system.md`.

Full record (decision 154): the complete text of each decision as made, with figures, sources and rejected options. The current rulings, which sessions read first, are in `../power.md`; the index of all decisions is `../INDEX.md`. Numbers are global and never reused. Text marked **[proposed]** still needs the user's confirmation.

## Decisions

60. Prototype power: certified DC power brick (24 or 48 V) into the master section, isolated DC-DC module to about ±20 V, LC filter, per-card linear regulation. Power switch, resettable fuse, reverse-polarity protection, locking DC connector, power LED. A linear external supply box can be considered for a product version. (Item 100 replaces the isolated DC-DC module with non-isolated on-board converters and fixes the brick at 24 V; the brick provides the isolation)
81. The power input section is a separate small power board (`hardware/power`) next to the master card: DC brick input, fuse, reverse-polarity protection, power switch and LED, isolated DC-DC to ±20 V and LC filter. It feeds the master card and both power chains with short power ribbons, keeping switching noise and its ground currents off the audio boards. The master card takes power like a channel card and regulates locally. (Item 100: the conversion is non-isolated; the brick provides the isolation)
100. Power board conversion and parts (refines items 60 and 81; power board schematic session 2026-10-07). Isolated DC-DC modules were dropped as too expensive (about $260 for a ±20 V series pair of Traco TEP modules, the only family checked that trims down to 20 V). The certified brick provides the isolation; the power board converts without isolation and the brick's negative is PGND, so the brick's DC output must float (settled by item 101: invariant 8)
    - **Brick:** 24 V, Class II (2-pin C8 inlet) with a floating output: Mean Well GSM120B24-R7B (120 W) for the prototype, GSM160B24-R7B (160 W) for 16 cards (item 145: GSM220B24-R7B for 16 cards). Same plug and pinout: R7B locking 4-pin power DIN (Kycon KPPX-4P type), pins 1 and 4 = +24 V, 2 and 3 = 0 V. Mean Well GST bricks were rejected: Class I, and the 120 and 160 W models tie −V to earth
    - **Jack:** Kycon KPJX-4S, PCB right-angle at the board's rear edge, 7.5 A per pin; the KPPX plug latches into it. Check polarity with a meter on the first brick (Mean Well's and Kycon's face drawings are rotated relative to each other)
    - **Converters:** two TPS54560 (60 V, 6.3 A minimum switch limit): a buck to +20 V and an inverting buck-boost to −20 V (TI SLVA317), about 2 A per rail. Both synchronised at 400 kHz in opposite phase from one on-board oscillator (74HC14); the timing resistors give 400 kHz if the clock fails. Light-load pulse skipping cannot be turned off, but the cards' quiescent load keeps both in fixed-frequency operation
    - **Input protection:** 4 A resettable fuse (LUTE 2920L400/30GR), SMBJ24A TVS (clamps 38.9 V, keeping the inverter IC's VIN + 20 V under 60 V), two SQJ457EP P-MOSFETs back to back: one blocks a reversed brick, the other turns on slowly (about 7 ms) so the converters' input capacitors charge gently. The power switch only drives the shared gate. Power LED on the switched side
    - **Output:** one LC filter per rail, shared by the three headers: 2.2 µH (Bourns SRP7028A), 330 µF polymer (Panasonic EEHZK1V331P), 100 µF electrolytic for damping, 4.7 µF ceramic. Simulated about 47 dB at 400 kHz at the filter, 63 dB at the cards, peaking about 4.4 dB near 4 kHz. One 2 A resettable fuse (BHFUSE BSMD1812-200-33V) per rail per header, six in all; the chain 2 header is fitted on the prototype too
    - **Power ribbon parts (SX-CONN-008):** Würth 61200821621 box header, 3 A per contact; Würth 61200823021 IDC socket, 1 A per contact (two contacts per rail). Neither is stocked at LCSC: hand-soldered and ordered from a distributor
    - **Full size (about 4 A per rail; 2026-10-10: 3.31 / 2.69 A at the sizing figure, 4.37 / 3.68 A at worst case, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`):** the +20 V buck probably carries it (thermal check needed); the −20 V inverter does not (about 7.5 A inductor current) and needs a controller with external MOSFETs, none found at LCSC. Settled with the full-size supply after the prototype cards are measured (decision 96). The 2920 input fuse does not scale to 6.5 A either
    - Component values and their derivation: `hardware/power/scripts/design.py`. Confirmed by the user 2026-10-08: UVLO start 21.1 V and stop 18.7 V on both converters (they wait for the input ramp; on the inverter the stop threshold is moot once running); oscillator 4.7 kΩ / 560 pF (275 to 445 kHz over the 74HC14's threshold spread; both loops cross over near 1.8 kHz, the inverter's peak inductor current stays about 4.6 A at 275 kHz); power switch and LED on the power board at the rear edge (switch part to choose); part-number categories L (inductors, also ferrite beads) and F (fuses). Parts still to choose: the 100 µF 50 V input bulk capacitor and the 10 pF C0G 100 V sync capacitor
    - **Start-up ramp** (power board session 2026-10-08; settled by Claude, the user delegates such technical choices): with the fixed 2.6 ms soft-start both converters charge about 1.1 mF per rail at their current limit, and the averaged model `hardware/power/scripts/startup_sim.py` puts the brick's input at 183 to 262 W peak (6.3 to 8.8 A switch limit, SLVSBN0C), over the hiccup threshold of the GSM120B24 (105 % of 120 W = 126 W) and of the GSM160B24 (168 W) for 1 to 2 ms. Starting the inverter 10 ms after the buck still exceeds 126 W at the typical limit. Fix: on each converter 47 nF C0G (SX-C-016) from the output through a 1N4148W (SX-D-003) into FB; the loop holds FB at 0.8 V, so the output rises as an RC with τ = 240 kΩ × 47 nF = 11.3 ms (19 V after about 40 ms) and the input peaks at about 54 W whatever the current limit. A 1 MΩ (SX-R-060) and a second 1N4148W clamp reset the node; in steady state the diode is off and the loop is unchanged. On the inverter the network is referred to its IC ground (capacitor from PGND, clamp and resistor to −20V_CONV). The GSM120B24 stays the prototype brick

187. Power board input EMI filter (power board session 2026-10-20; refines item 100; the user chose the recommendation, and the brick-side capacitor below was added on the calculation and reported). Asked how the TPS54560s' EMI is handled, the review found the output side covered (one 400 kHz clock, both converters synchronised in opposite phase, so no beat in the audio band; the second-stage LC filter per rail; the separate board and PGND; the input ceramics tight at each IC over a bottom PGND plane, shielded inductors), but nothing between the brick lead and the converters except C103 and the ceramics: the converters' pulsed 400 kHz input current ran out through the four internal wires and the 1.8 m brick lead, the board's largest antenna, and back through the brick to the mains. Ruling: a pi filter after the soft-start MOSFET Q102: C108 (4.7 uF 100 V X7S 1210, SX-C-018, the converters' input ceramic) on the brick side, L101 (2.2 uH Bourns SRP7028A-2R2M, SX-L-001, the output filters' inductor; 8 A rms, 14 A saturation, 20 mOhm) in series, then C103 and the converter ceramics. New net VIN_PRE: Q102 drain, the Miller capacitor C102 (the ramp follows the drain), C108 pin 1, L101 pin 1; VIN_SW starts at L101 pin 2. No new part type, so no new fee; about EUR 1-2 per build (`tools/costs.py estimate`: EUR 793 / 1,028 / 1,590 for 4 / 8 / 16 channels, from 792 / 1,027 / 1,588). Calculation (2026-10-20, hand model: brick lead and internal wires 0.5-3 uH with 50 mOhm, the brick's output an AC short, converter ceramics about 15 uF after DC bias, C103 ESR 0.05-0.4 Ohm, not taken from the UHE datasheet): 400 kHz ripple current in the lead relative to the converters' input ripple is about -44 dB today (-34 to -50 dB over the lead range); L101 alone gains almost nothing (-44 dB with a 1.5 uH lead) because the 100 nF C101 at the fuse resonates with the lead near 400 kHz; with C108 about -80 dB (-70 to -86 dB over the lead range), about 36 dB better. The filter's output impedance peaks at 0.2-0.36 Ohm near 7-20 kHz, far below the converters' negative input resistance (about 5.3 Ohm at 108 W from the brick), damped by C103's ESR, so no extra damping part. C108 sits behind Q102, so it is charged by the soft start (no hot-plug inrush into a bare ceramic). Layout: F101 stands upright in the free rear corner, D101 and C101 turned, Q101 and Q102 8.7 mm to the rear, L101 between Q102 and C103, C108 and C102 below L101 on a VIN_PRE copper area, the VIN_SW spine from L101's output; TP102 moved between F101 and J101. `placement.py` now takes the parts and every pad's net from the schematic netlist (new parts added from their library footprints). Board rebuilt by `build_pcb.sh`: DRC 0 violations, 0 unconnected, schematic parity. Rejected: L101 alone (the recommendation as first worded: about 0 dB in the model), a ferrite bead only (little effect at 400 kHz), a footprint with a 0 Ohm bridge fitted only if the bench needs it (same board space, a decision deferred without gain), and nothing until the bench (a later fix means a board revision). The filter sits before the full-size input's 9.2 A: the full-size module is a new design (decision 185).
