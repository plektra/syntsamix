# System and chain decisions

System scope, signal levels, buses, cross-board functions and the chain between cards (audio and power ribbons, grounding).

Project-wide rules (jack sizes, RoHS, shared jack part, cost principle: items 118, 122, 132, 135) are in `system-rules.md`. Full record (decision 154): the complete text of each decision as made, with figures, sources and rejected options. The current rulings, which sessions read first, are in `../system.md`; the index of all decisions is `../INDEX.md`. Numbers are global and never reused. Text marked **[proposed]** still needs the user's confirmation.

## Decisions

1. Pro audio mixer for connecting synthesizers and instruments in electronic music live performances
2. Modular by design
3. Prototype: 4 stereo channels
13. The compressor processes a dedicated compressor bus; each channel assigns to main or compressor bus
14. AUX sends and returns are stereo by default; the design must also support mono inputs and therefore mono AUX sends
25. Inputs accept hot Eurorack modular levels (up to ±12 V peak) as well as line levels, without an external attenuator
27. All jacks on the mixer are 6.3 mm (channel inputs: 2x 6.3 mm TRS, L/MONO and R; also AUX sends and returns, sidechain input, outputs and headphones). No 3.5 mm jacks on the prototype; Eurorack users connect with 3.5 mm to 6.3 mm cables. Replaces the earlier plans of 3.5 mm alongside 6.3 mm and of 3.5 mm only. Other jack sizes can come later through the interchangeable input module (backlog) (jack-size rule replaced by item 118)
28. PFL is in the prototype: a button per channel (post-filter, pre-fader, pre-mute) feeding a stereo cue bus; headphones switch to cue automatically while any PFL is active; PFL-active LED. No solo-in-place
30. Channel strips can be added one at a time; each stereo channel card has no direct dependency on other channel cards
32. Budget: relatively tight; prefer cost-effective parts where the audible difference is small
34. Expansion: chainable bus from the start, so the system can grow without a fixed slot count (form chosen in item 38)
38. Chain unit: per card. Each channel card has identical IN and OUT connectors wired straight through, linked to its neighbour by short ribbon cables; the master card sits at one end. No backplane PCB
39. Practical maximum: 16 channel cards
40. Chain power: unregulated about ±20 V nominal (raised from ±18 V so the far card's regulators stay out of dropout after ripple and cable drop); each card regulates its own ±15 V. Audio ground and power ground use separate conductors
41. Separate ribbons: a 34-pin IDC audio ribbon (buses, logic lines, interleaved audio grounds, spares) and a keyed 8-pin IDC power ribbon (2× V+, 2× V−, 4× ground). The 10- and 16-pin sizes are avoided so a Eurorack power cable cannot be plugged in
42. Chain pinouts, signal definitions and grounding rules as in `CHAIN.md`. Sidechain bus is mono. Power enters the chain at the master card, which is also the only point where audio ground and power ground join (since items 80 and 81 power enters at the separate power board; the AGND-PGND join stays on the master card)
46. Confirmed in one batch: analog signal path; +4 dBu nominal, about +20 dBu internal maximum, +24 dBu only at balanced outputs; performance targets in SPEC.md section 5; DC-coupled receiver inputs with AC coupling about 3 Hz after the receiver; click-free mute via the VCA; fader bottom drives the VCA past -100 dB (fully off); sidechain LPF 40 to 500 Hz, 12 dB/octave, with bypass; channel independence rules (identical slots, master-side bus summing, wired-OR logic, no shared parts, per-card regulation); master level through an SSI2162 VCA and linear pot; headphone volume knob; balanced outputs on 6.3 mm TRS; external supply delivering unregulated about ±20 V
61. Grounding: jack sleeves to their card's AGND; FR4 panels isolate jacks from the frame; frame bonded to the star point at the master card only, with a ground-lift switch (RC when lifted). Chain connectors are 2.54 mm shrouded keyed box headers (2x17, 2x4) with 28 AWG IDC ribbon; part numbers chosen during the schematic
64. Soft clipping after each channel's input trim stage and on the master bus output, onset about 6 dB below the internal maximum (about +14 dBu). Inspired by the Intellijel Jellymix
66. Sidechain ducking through the channel VCAs (inspired by the Boredbrain Xcelon SL): the master card turns the filtered sidechain into an envelope (THRESHOLD, DEPTH, DECAY; fast fixed attack) on chain line SC_ENV (was SPARE1); each channel has a DUCK button that adds it to its VCA control; AUX returns get a DUCK switch too. Complements the compressor bus
69. Stereo button functions are switched electronically: each latching button has two contact sets, one driving a logic line into a DG413 quad analog switch (Vishay, SOIC-16; 2 NO + 2 NC, so one chip selects between two stereo signals) and one lighting its LED. Audio never runs through mechanical contacts. Each channel card makes a +5 V logic supply locally (for the DG413 VL pins). Refines decision 54 (refined by item 152: a button pole may switch a DC control voltage directly)
80. Power ribbon current (open since item 78) is solved by injecting power in groups of 8 cards: chain 1 feeds cards 1 to 8, chain 2 runs a separate power ribbon to card 9 and on to card 16 (no cable between cards 8 and 9). With the separate power board (item 81) both chains start at the power board, which also feeds the master card through its own ribbon, so no single ribbon carries more than 8 cards. About 0.53 A per pin at full size (restated by item 96: 0.76 A per pin under its sizing rule; 0.74 A after items 102 and 103, 0.70 A after item 123, figures in `ARCHITECTURE.md`). Channel cards are unchanged; the 4-channel prototype uses only chain 1
96. Power sizing rule (system validation 2026-10-06, finding 2; `tools/system_power_budget.py`, which runs both cards' `power_budget.py`). A card's own parts (regulators, heatsinks, its ribbon segment) are sized for its worst case: every IC at datasheet maximum, every LED lit, full signal load. Parts shared by many cards (the DC-DC, the ribbon pins at the start of a chain) are sized for IC quiescent current at typical × 1.5 plus the use-dependent loads (LEDs, relays, signal, headphones) at their maximum, because datasheet maxima do not all occur together across many parts while the use-dependent loads can. Per rail at 20 V raw: channel card 189 / 157 mA (+15 / −15 V), master 597 / 485 mA. Prototype (4 cards and the master): 1.35 / 1.11 A, about 49 W; the prototype DC-DC gets about 2 A per rail. Full size (16 cards and the master): 3.6 / 3.0 A, about 132 W, the supply rating for 16 cards. Power ribbon: 0.76 A per pin with 8 cards per chain, so decision 80 stands provided the chosen IDC headers, sockets and cable are rated at least 1 A per contact (checked when they are chosen). The 1.5 factor is a judgment: replace it with bench measurements of the prototype cards before a full-size supply is bought. (Since item 100 "the DC-DC" means the power board's own converters, and the full-size step is sizing or revising them rather than buying a module. The figures above are as decided; after items 102 and 103 the script gives slightly lower ones, kept current in `ARCHITECTURE.md`)
97. SC_ENV scale and ducking circuit (system validation 2026-10-06, finding 1; refines items 66, 72 and 90; changes `CHAIN.md`). SC_ENV is negative: 0 V = no ducking, −1 V = 10 dB of gain reduction, DEPTH 0 to −4 V = 0 to 40 dB (decision 90's range unchanged). Each channel card and each master AUX return feeds the DUCK-switched SC_ENV as a current into the control summer's virtual earth (VSUM) through 30k1 against the 10k feedback resistor (0.332 V/V, 10.06 dB/V at the SSI2162's −33 mV/dB), and VPLUS goes to AGND, so the ducking no longer depends on the fader position or the superdiode states (the old non-inverting input gave 4.6 to 10 dB/V). The master drives SC_ENV with a unity inverter in place of the follower (drawn instead as a negative DEPTH voltage and the existing follower, item 104), its feedback taken after the 100 Ω series resistor so the line stays low impedance with up to 18 loads of 30k1 (about 1.7 kΩ, 2.4 mA at −4 V); otherwise the depth would change with the number of ducking cards. A Schottky diode from SC_ENV to AGND on the master limits a positive fault excursion to about +0.3 V (at most about 3 dB of boost); a disconnected line gives no ducking. Ducking passes through the channel's 10 ms control smoothing (R215, C224), so the effective attack is about 10 ms instead of the master's 3 ms; accepted, to be heard on the breadboard. Positive SC_ENV through an inverter on every card was rejected (18 extra stages); a documented fader-dependent scale was rejected (channels would duck differently) (refined by item 153: 30 kΩ instead of 30k1)
98. Logic returns to PGND (system validation 2026-10-06, finding 4; refines item 42, changes `CHAIN.md`): the open-collector PFL_ACT drivers (channel Q301, master Q501) have their emitters on PGND, and every button pull-down (100 kΩ from a button control line) goes to PGND instead of AGND, on both cards. The PFL LED and pull-up current (about 2.5 mA) then flows out on the PFL_ACT conductor and back through the power ribbons, never in AGND, so invariant 4 holds without exceptions. The DG41x GND pins stay on AGND; the millivolts between PGND and AGND do not matter at 5 V logic levels. No parts added. Documenting the exception instead was rejected
101. Floating supply (`/system power conversion` 2026-10-08; follows item 100, refines items 42 and 61, changes `CHAIN.md` and adds invariant 8). Whatever feeds the power ribbons must have a DC output that floats from mains earth: today the Class II 24 V brick of item 100; a future external linear supply box, an internal mains supply or the full-size supply must keep it. PGND is earthed nowhere; the only ground reference inside the mixer is the star point on the master card, and the mixer takes its earth reference from connected gear (as most desktop audio gear with an external Class II adapter does). The ground-lift switch (item 61) stays as it is: the frame connects only to the star point, directly or through the RC when lifted, which matters when the frame touches something earthed (for example rack ears). The prototype keeps the Class II brick; an internal mains supply (Class I: safety earth hard-wired to the chassis, the lift moving between signal ground and chassis) and an external linear supply box are backlog items. Rejected: an isolated converter stage on the power board (the cost item 100 removed) and a rule recorded only as a power-board part requirement (lost at the next supply change)
152. Buttons may switch DC control voltages (`/system` 2026-10-10; refines item 69, confirms the board change of item 142). Audio never runs through a button's contacts: stereo audio switching stays on DG413s as item 69 says. A button pole may switch a DC control voltage directly (today MUTE: −15 V, and DUCK: SC_ENV, into the channel's control summer) when both hold: (a) the circuit does not depend on the contact sequence: the released throw is left open and a hold resistor ties the switched line to ground when off, so a make-before-break contact cannot short a rail or a shared chain line; (b) a smoothing ramp after the contact (the 10 ms control summer on the channel) keeps the switching click-free and filters contact bounce. The contacts then carry only wetting-level DC (at most about 0.5 mA). The prototype-scope line in the root `CLAUDE.md` ("audio never runs through a button: buttons carry logic, LED and DC control currents") is confirmed by the user with this item. Rejected: restoring the channel's U206 DG413 (about €1.80 per card, no audible or stage benefit, decision 135) and changing only the scope wording (item 69 would still contradict the board, and the next board could switch a voltage without conditions (a) and (b))
153. SC_ENV load 30 kΩ (`/system` 2026-10-10; refines item 97, changes `CHAIN.md` and the SC_ENV chain line). A channel card or AUX return with DUCK on loads SC_ENV with 30 kΩ (E24, SX-R-050, JLCPCB basic) into its control summer's virtual earth instead of 30.1 kΩ: channel R217, master R232 and R332. Against the 10 k feedback the scale becomes 0.333 V/V, 10.10 dB/V at the SSI2162's −33 mV/dB (was 10.07), so the ducking at the full −4 V DEPTH is 0.13 dB deeper; inaudible, on the may-relax side of decision 135. The master's SC_ENV driver sees about 1.67 kΩ with 18 loads (was 1.7 kΩ), within the low-impedance drive of item 97. Removes the last off-E24 value on both boards (decision 134) and one extended JLCPCB type, about $3 per order (decision 137); SX-R-087 is no longer used once both boards are redrawn. Rejected: keeping 30k1 for the exact 10 dB per −1 V figure (one fee type on every order for 0.03 dB per volt)
175. Multi-drop chain cables in 8-card sections (`/system` 2026-10-17; refines items 38, 80, 109, 121 and 162; changes `CHAIN.md`, `MECHANICAL.md` and `ARCHITECTURE.md`). The user dropped the cable mock-up of item 109 ("I will not do any cable mockups", 2026-10-17), which was to fix the header orientation, pin-1 crimping through the U-loop, the jumper length and the underside keep-out. On Claude's recommendation the user replaced the daisy chain (identical IN and OUT headers per card wired straight through, U-loop jumpers between neighbours) by multi-drop flat cables in sections of 8 cards, the audio sections joined by a coupler board, one power cable per group. (1) Cards: every channel card carries one audio header (Würth 61203421621, 2×17) and one power header (61200821621, 2×4) on its underside, long axis front to back, pin 1 toward the front (stripe along the cable's front edge), key on the same side on every card; their distances from the front and their position across the card are chosen by the channel card layout and recorded in `MECHANICAL.md`; every card is the same, so the sockets sit at the 35 mm strip pitch. (2) Audio cable: one standard section, 34-way flat cable with 8 card sockets (Würth 61203423021) about 37 mm apart on the cable (35 mm pitch plus about 2 mm so the cable bows instead of pulling; marked on a paper crimping guide) and an end socket at each end. The right end plugs into the master card's audio header, which sits on the cards' header line in the same orientation; a second section (cards 9-16) joins the left end of the first through a coupler board, a small passive PCB with two shrouded 2×17 headers in the same orientation, pin n to pin n, held in the cable run under the strips by a cable tie or a printed clip (never on the bottom cover). A free end socket stays unplugged. End-segment lengths are set at the channel and master layouts. (3) Power cable: one section per group of 8 cards (item 80), 10-way flat cable peeled to 8 along its length (SX-WIRE-003), a feed socket at the power board and 8 card sockets; chain 1's feed segment runs under the master section, chain 2's about 0.5 m along the rear floor (item 162); the master keeps its own two-socket power ribbon. (4) Pin 1 and orientation: all sockets on a section are crimped on the same cable face with the stripe on one edge, so identical header placement on every card, the master and the coupler gives pin 1 on pin 1 everywhere; nothing is left to a mock-up. At card sockets the cable passes straight through (no strain-relief fold); end sockets may use the strain relief (check the Würth drawing). (5) Current: each card's socket contacts carry only that card's current (about 0.11 A per contact at a card's worst case, two contacts per rail); the cable conductors and the feed socket at the power board carry the whole group's, 0.68 A per contact at the sizing figure and 0.92 A at worst case (`tools/system_power_budget.py`), so the 1 A Würth socket remains the limit at the feed socket only. The 3M 3365's current rating and the 28 AWG fit in the Würth sockets are checked from datasheets (a `part-facts` lookup) before the first order, or a cable with a stated rating replaces it. (6) Keep-out: no underside parts in a band across the whole card width over each header's length plus 15 mm front and back (socket body and fingers on the latch; item 121's 15 mm, now final); the cable runs below at the sockets' depth; the channel card layout checks header, socket and cable depth against the sloped floor's inside height at the header distance (item 162). (7) Prototype: one audio section and one chain 1 power section, each with 8 card sockets of which 4 are used, and the master power ribbon: about 19 sockets, hand-crimped by the user with an IDC press or a smooth-jawed vise; the coupler board is drawn only before a build of more than 8 cards. Gains: no orientation or U-loop question; any card can be pulled (even from the middle) and the others keep their buses and power, which suits invariant 1 better; two headers per card instead of four and no straight-through traces across the 33 mm card; less current per card socket. Cost: growing past one section takes a second section and the coupler instead of one more jumper pair; spare sockets cost about €1-2 each. Item 109's reason for rejecting multi-drop (a different cable for each card count) is answered by the fixed 8-card section. Rejected: keeping the daisy chain and settling item 109 on paper (Claude's first recommendation; keeps the U-loop and four headers per card); ready-made IDC jumpers (hard to find at about 70 mm in 2×4 and 2×17); a male IDC plug on the section end (less common, keying and RoHS source not checked); a double-ended pin strip between two sockets (unkeyed, can be plugged reversed or offset)

## Rationale notes

- Why PFL in the prototype: without it, trimming a channel by ear happens in the main mix where the audience hears it. Tapping before the VCA makes PFL pre-mute, so a channel can be prepared while muted. Solo-in-place was rejected because an accidental press silences the main mix.
- Why separate power and audio ribbons: with 16 cards, the card nearest the supply carries every card's current (about 3.6 A per rail at full size, decision 96). A separate power cable keeps that current, the rectifier ripple and switching spikes away from the sensitive mix bus lines, and can be heavier or fed from both ends. Separate ground conductors matter most: shared ground with power return current would put hum on the buses.
- Why no submix per block of cards: the noise is dominated by the sum of the channels' own noise, which grouping cannot change; a local submix lowers the total by only about 0.3 dB at 16 channels (estimate) and would break per-card independence.
- Why keep PFL with channel meters: the meter shows level, PFL lets the performer hear a channel (patch, tuning, filter, hum, timing) before the audience does. The channel meters take over trimming, so the master meter no longer needs to switch to cue.
- Why a compressor bus: classic pumping keys the music from the kick. On a master-bus compressor the kick would duck itself; a separate bus lets the kick stay on main and trigger the compressor.
