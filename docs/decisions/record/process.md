79. Assembly at JLCPCB (or PCBWay) for the prototype is planned around the LCSC stock check (`docs/lcsc-check.csv`, `tools/lcsc_check.py`). To cut extended-part setup fees, non-critical resistors use JLC basic values: bus resistors 22 kΩ (was 22.1 kΩ), the VCA input network 100 Ω (was 110 Ω), the cutoff offset injection 470 kΩ (was 499 kΩ, range still about ±32 mV) and the regulator dividers 200 Ω / 2.2 kΩ (15.1 V; was 120 Ω / 1.33 kΩ). 116. RoHS compliance is a strict requirement (confirmed by the user 2026-10-09, during `/board channel-card mechanical parts`; for EU compliance). Every component on every board, and the boards themselves, must comply with the RoHS directive 2011/65/EU as amended by Delegated Directive (EU) 2015/863 (ten restricted substances); compliance through an Annex III exemption counts as compliant. A part may be chosen only when its maker or supplier states RoHS compliance (datasheet mark, declaration, or the distributor's RoHS field), and the source is recorded in the Notes column of `docs/parts.csv` as `RoHS: <source>`. Parts without such a statement (for example unbranded synth-DIY pots, knobs and jacks) are not chosen until compliance is confirmed. Fabrication and assembly are lead-free: lead-free HASL or ENIG board finish and lead-free assembly at JLCPCB (not the default leaded HASL), lead-free solder for hand soldering. The parts already registered are audited against this rule (all boards) (invariant 9, standard passives and the audit: item 122 in `system-rules.md`)
124. A maker's general RoHS declaration covers that maker's standard parts (confirmed by the user 2026-10-09, `/system`; refines item 116). Taiwan Alpha's RoHS II declaration (2020-10-26, signed; 2011/65/EU with 2015/863, all ten substances) covers "our standard models or series", so every standard Alpha potentiometer counts as RoHS compliant under item 116, whichever reseller sells it (Thonk, Tayda), provided the part is genuine Alpha. This clears the channel pots CUTOFF, TRIM, AUX (Thonk, Alpha order codes RD901F-40-15K-B50K-0057, RD902F-40-15K-A100K-0057, RD902F-40-15K-A10K-0057) and RESONANCE (Tayda, RD901F-40-15K-C10K), and gives the master card's Alpha pots their source once a standard model is chosen. Supplier declarations received by email are kept in `docs/rohs/`, which is git-ignored: they are the suppliers' documents and not ours to publish in the public repository; `parts.csv` names the document, its date and scope. The same reading applies to other makers' general declarations
133. Through-hole parts are hand-soldered by the user; JLCPCB places SMD parts only (confirmed by the user 2026-10-09, cost review; refines item 79). This covers the panel parts (pots, faders, latching switches, 6.3 mm and 3.5 mm jacks), the chain and input headers, jumper headers, trimmers, relays, the master's TO-220 regulators and heatsinks, and the power board's radial capacitor and DC jack. No through-hole assembly is ordered, so JLCPCB charges no through-hole set-up or per-joint fees; the order files list these parts for the user's own purchase. Footprints keep hand-solderable pads (no paste-in-hole assumptions).
134. Component values come from the E24 series, and each board owns its value selection (confirmed by the user 2026-10-09, cost review; refines items 79 and 110). Resistor and capacitor values are chosen from E24 (capacitors preferably E12 or E6, where the JLCPCB basic library is widest). A value outside E24 stays only where the circuit needs a ratio or accuracy that E24 values cannot give, singly or as a pair of E24 parts; each such value is listed in its board's `STATUS.md` (section "Component values") with its references and reason. The board's own sessions are responsible: they apply the rule when drawing, review their list before layout, and record each exception or change there. Reason: every value outside the JLCPCB basic library costs a $3 extended-part fee per type per order (item 110), and off-series values are harder to source for a product. Item 79's exact values (filter scaling, fader law, meter ladder) stand until their board's review moves or justifies them. The rule is in each board's `CLAUDE.md`, "Component values".
136. Cost control and realized costs (confirmed by the user 2026-10-09). (1) `/cost <scope>` runs the `cost-controller` sub-agent (`.claude/agents/cost-controller.md`), read-only on the design like the validator: it prices the BOMs, checks them against item 135 and the E24 rule (item 134), explores new cost-saving scenarios, and writes `docs/reviews/<date>-cost-<scope>.md` with each lever's saving, trade-off, the side of item 135 it is on, and the decisions it would touch. Its levers are proposals; accepted ones become topics in a `/board` or `/system` session. (2) `tools/costs.py` makes the estimate repeatable from the schematics: `estimate --channels N`, `drivers`, `unpriced`; its inputs are `docs/lcsc-check.csv`, `docs/costs/prices.csv` (prices for parts LCSC does not sell, with source and date) and `docs/costs/overheads.csv` (rates, PCB, panel, JLCPCB fee, frame and knob figures). The ROADMAP's cost figures come from it. (3) Realized costs: every order and payment is recorded with `tools/costs.py ledger add` (date, supplier, order reference, category, board, amount and currency, converted to EUR); `tools/costs.py ledger` totals them by category, board and supplier. The ledger `docs/costs/ledger.csv` is private: git-ignored (the repository is public) and kept in the main checkout, which the tool finds from any worktree. (4) `/handoff` notes the cost effect of a session that changed parts.
137. Prefer JLCPCB's fee-free parts (confirmed by the user 2026-10-10: "highly prefer the standard parts from LCSC in order to avoid extended component costs"; refines items 79, 110 and 134). For every SMD part JLCPCB places, choose in this order: (1) a JLCPCB basic part; (2) a JLCPCB "preferred" part (an extended part that carries no extended-part fee); (3) another LCSC extended part ($3 fee per type per order); (4) a part LCSC does not stock (consigned or hand-placed). A lower choice is allowed only where no fee-free part meets the need under item 135 (live device: accuracy the audience cannot hear may be traded; the "must stay" list may not), and it is recorded with its reason in the board's `STATUS.md`. Same function counts: a basic part from another maker (for example ST's TL072CDT for a TL072) is fine once its datasheet confirms pinout, package and limits. Resistor values follow item 134 (E24), and the chosen E24 value should be one the basic or preferred library holds (most 1 % 0805 E24 values are; 110 Ω, for example, was not on 2026-10-10). Through-hole parts are hand-soldered (item 133) and bought wherever they are cheapest. `tools/lcsc_check.py` records the class as basic, preferred or extended; `tools/costs.py` counts basic and preferred as fee-free, and `tools/costs.py extended` lists the types that still cost a fee.
151. Models per role and a lighter session start, to cut token use (confirmed by the user 2026-10-10; refines item 136 and the workflow in `docs/WORKING-WITH-CLAUDE.md`). Design sessions (`/board`, `/system`) stay on Opus. The sub-agents carry a fixed model in their `.claude/agents/` files: `architect`, `cost-controller` and `validator` on Sonnet; `/validate <scope> deep` runs the validator on Opus, used for a full board before layout and before a PCB order and for `system` before the first full order; likewise `/cost <scope> deep` runs the cost controller on Opus before layout, before each order and for `system` when new levers are wanted, while plain `/cost` (re-pricing, rule checks) stays on Sonnet. New read-only helper agents take the bounded jobs that used to start a general-purpose agent on the session's model: `part-facts` (datasheet facts with citations, Sonnet, because part reports have come back wrong before), `build-check` (reference build, `check_netlist.py` and ERC, Haiku), `sim-run` (ngspice runs against `results.txt`, Haiku) and `stock-check` (LCSC stock, class and prices, Haiku). Models are not switched in the middle of a session, since the switch drops the prompt cache; a fresh session for a phase with nothing left to decide may start on Sonnet. `/board` no longer reads all of `INDEX.md` and `ARCHITECTURE.md`: only the index's area table, rules, the board's rows, **[proposed]** rows and the open items, and the architecture's invariants and open system items (budgets and chain lines when the phase touches power, ribbons, signal levels or the panel, and always for the power board). Refined the same day (confirmed by the user): (1) `/board` reads the board's decision files in full only when the phase changes the circuit (schematic, breadboard, simulation); for PCB, mock-up, ordering and cost phases it reads the decisions named in the status files or the topic, by number, and reads in full before any circuit change. (2) Each board's `STATUS.md` keeps only the session state (where it stands, waiting, next, For /system); checks before layout and on delivery, checked part facts, cost-cut levers and the component value list (items 134 and 137 named `STATUS.md` for these) move to `hardware/<board>/CHECKLISTS.md`, read for layout, assembly, ordering and cost work and by the validator and cost controller. (3) `/handoff` runs the `architect` reviewer only when the changes touch a contract, a budget, `parts.csv`, a chain, bus or input-header script, a power budget script, or a decision that changes a signal level, rail, load or panel part.
154. Decision log in two tiers, to cut what sessions read (confirmed by the user 2026-10-10: "go ahead" on the recommendation; refines item 151 and the log rules in `INDEX.md`). The area files had grown to about 122 KB (140 KB with the index), a `/board channel-card schematic` start-up read about 36 KB of decision text, and recent entries ran to 1-2 KB each (measurements, prices and stock dates, rejected options, delegation quotes, netlist and ERC results), while a decision's current state was often spread over a refinement chain (12 → 50 → 71, 72 → 102 → 140 → 142, 65 → 73). (1) `docs/decisions/<area>.md` now holds the current ruling of each decision: what holds today, refinements folded in, at most a one-clause reason where it prevents reopening, references to the decisions that set it. (2) `docs/decisions/record/<area>.md` holds the full text unchanged (moved with `git mv`, so `git log --follow` keeps the history), with the tables, rationale notes and availability notes; it is read when a number needs its figures or sources, before reopening a decision, and by the validator and cost controller. (3) A new decision is written in both: the full entry appended to the record, the short ruling appended to the area file, and every short entry it refines rewritten to the new state (the record entries of refined decisions are not edited). (4) `tools/check_decisions.py` checks that `INDEX.md`, the short files and the record files list the same numbers, each in one area. (5) `/board`, `/system`, `/handoff` and the agents read the short files; the short files and the record were checked against each other once by the validator. Rejected: archiving superseded decisions only (little saving, since the long entries are the live ones) and trimming in place with git as the only record (the rationale would be hard to reach, inviting decisions to be reopened). Result: the area files sessions read fall from about 122 KB to about 47 KB (the channel card's two files from about 36 KB to about 12 KB)

191. PCBA supplier stays JLCPCB (confirmed by the user 2026-10-21 after `/cost` compared AISLER and PCBWay: "let's stick with jlcpcb"; refines 79). Report: `docs/reviews/2026-10-21-cost-system-pcba-suppliers.md`. The three assembled designs hold 230 distinct SMD part types (channel card 81, master 116, power 33); JLCPCB charges only the 28 extended types ($3.07 each; whole fee line EUR 144 at 4 channels). AISLER charges per distinct part: EUR 3.75 to 7.50 per type (2022-2023 posts, current rates behind its upload calculator) gives EUR 863 to 1,725 in part fees alone, about EUR 720 to 1,580 more per 4-channel build; its bare boards would also cost about EUR 136 against about EUR 60. PCBWay publishes no fee schedule (quote per project; an old user report suggests about 2x parts markup), so it could not be bounded. Rejected for the prototype: AISLER (cost; its advantages are an EU invoice, no customs and 6-9 working days) and PCBWay (no evidence it is cheaper). Open for production: re-quote AISLER, PCBWay and JLCPCB Standard PCBA (about EUR 320 loading per order for 230 types) at 16 cards and about 50 consoles. Follow-up the same day on JLCPCB's "Pre-reflow Soldering" line ($0.016 per joint, Economic and Standard, price page 2026-09-09): no JLCPCB page defines it; the evidence (through-hole parts normally wave, selective or hand soldered after reflow; pin-in-paste requested in the order remarks with through-hole pads on the paste layer; the rate matches manual assembly at $0.0164 rather than SMT at $0.0016) points to a charge on through-hole joints soldered in the reflow oven, not on every SMT joint. The cost review's worst case (about EUR 124 at 4 channels if every joint were charged) is therefore taken as zero while orders follow 133: SMD-only BOM and position files, no pin-in-paste remark, no through-hole pads on the paste layer; ask JLCPCB support if the line still appears on a quote. Not modelled in `overheads.csv`.
