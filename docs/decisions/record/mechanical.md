# Mechanical decisions

Frame, panels, strip layout and front-panel parts.

Full record (decision 154): the complete text of each decision as made, with figures, sources and rejected options. The current rulings, which sessions read first, are in `../mechanical.md`; the index of all decisions is `../INDEX.md`. Numbers are global and never reused. Text marked **[proposed]** still needs the user's confirmation.

## Decisions

55. Channel strip layout follows the signal flow top to bottom: trim, cutoff, resonance, filter bypass, AUX 1, AUX 2, SC send and compressor bus buttons, PFL, mute, then meter beside the fader (item 106 puts the cutoff CV jack on its own spot above CUTOFF and sets the strip envelope) (meter: printed bezel in one panel slot, item 119)
59. Mechanical format: desktop unit; one PCB plus FR4 top panel (about 35 mm) per strip; rear-panel PCB-mount jacks; aluminium rail frame cut to length with side cheeks; chain ribbons under the strips
75. Buttons: plain latching DPDT PCB switches, CW Industries GPBS850N (Electrokit 41012905, about 0.57 € each at 25+), with 3D-printed translucent caps lit from below by an 0805 LED beside each switch. Latching mechanics keep every button state through a power cut, which is a must (for example a power cut during a gig). Illuminated latching switches were rejected on cost (about 4 $ each for the cheapest suitable part, E-Switch LP4). Pressed = pins 2-3 and 5-6 closed (datasheet), so the logic line is on pin 3 and the LED on pin 6. Erratum 2026-10-10: the MPN was recorded as GPBS850N, taken from the datasheet Electrokit links from 41012905; that datasheet is the momentary twin (Electrokit 41015527, Mouser "DPDT Non-Latching ON-(ON)"). The latching part Electrokit sells as 41012905 ("on-on, inte återfjädrande") is CW Industries GPBS850L (datasheet Rev 2, 2014: same body, pin grid and pressed = 2-3, 5-6 contacts; plunger 4.0 mm wide instead of 3.8). The decision itself is unchanged; MPN, footprint name and parts.csv corrected
86. Meters use small round 3 mm LEDs, on the channel cards (8 segments) and on the master (12 segments per side). Ready-made segmented bar graphs and square LED stacks were judged too bulky; the bar-graph sample is dropped from the second Electrokit order (channel meter refined by item 119: 0805 LEDs under a printed bezel)
106. Strip envelope and mechanical contract (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-08; refines items 55, 59 and 68). Strip pitch 35.0 mm, top panel strip 34.8 mm, channel card PCB at most 33.0 mm wide, top panel at most 320 mm deep (the panel column sums to about 300 to 320 mm, an estimate until the layout). The cutoff CV jack moves from beside CUTOFF to its own spot centred on the strip directly above it (about 18 mm of depth): at 35 mm a 6.3 mm plug body (12 to 15 mm across) beside the knob would leave about 15 mm for the most-played knob. Panels stay FR4 (aluminium considered and set aside for now; still a product-version option). The mechanical numbers boards must fit live in a new contract, `docs/MECHANICAL.md`, changed only by a confirmed decision. Rejected: 40 mm strips with the jack beside the knob (unit and every board and panel about 14 % larger), and 35 mm with a small (about 12 mm) CUTOFF knob beside the jack
107. Panel stack (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-08; refines item 59, adds to `MECHANICAL.md`). The top of the PCB sits 10.0 mm below the underside of the 1.6 mm FR4 panel, set by the Alpha 9 mm pots (RD901F-40 and RD902F-40: 10 mm body, M7×0.75 bushing 5 mm long, shaft L measured from the body top; Alpha drawing SLH-211-414 and 9 mm catalog page 5). The pots fix the panel with washer (0.4 mm) and nut (2 mm): 4.0 of the 5 mm thread. The fader (Bourns PTA6043, datasheet Rev. 12/14: frame top 6.5 mm above the PCB, two M2 tapped holes 71 mm apart, DP lever 15 mm above the frame) is screwed to the panel with two M2 screws through 3.5 mm spacers, so the lever stands about 10 mm above the panel and the push of a live set goes into the panel, not the solder joints. Assembly order (the user's rule): the fader is screwed to the panel first and only then soldered to the PCB. Parts on the top side under the panel are at most 9 mm tall (1 mm clearance). Rejected: tall parts on the underside (hand soldering on the bottom, in the way of the ribbons) and a taller gap on standoffs with pots not fixed to the panel (knob forces into the PCB only); a fader held only by its PCB pins (underside parts allowed by items 112 and 121)
108. Card fixing (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-08; refines items 59 and 106). Each top panel rests on the front and back 2020 rails, covering each rail's full 20 mm top face, and is fixed with two M3 screws per end (four per panel), about 24 mm apart across the strip, into drop-in M3 T-nuts in the rail's top slot (slot centre 10 mm from the rail edge). The card hangs from its panel by the pots and the fader. The 320 mm of item 106 is the control area between the rails' inner faces; the panel is then at most 360 mm long, and the PCB fits between the rails, at most 318 mm deep (1 mm clearance to each rail; at 10 mm under the panel it cannot pass over a 20 mm deep rail). Rejected: one screw per end (less stiff against twist from buttons near the edges) and standoffs to a bottom plate (a full-width plate and hardware in every slot) (screws per end replaced by item 120)
109. Chain header placement (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-08; refines items 38 and 59). The master section sits at the right end of the unit, with the power board beside it. On every channel card the audio (2×17) and power (2×4) IN headers run along the left edge and the OUT headers along the right edge, on the underside, long axis front to back, all at one distance from the front; the channel card layout chooses that distance (in the rear half, clear of the fader) and records it in `MECHANICAL.md`, and the master card's headers follow it. Neighbours are linked by identical short jumper cables in a U-loop under the gap between card n's OUT and card n+1's IN (about 12 mm apart, about 60 to 80 mm of cable). Before the first PCB order a mock-up (two box headers about 12 mm apart on scrap board, a real 34-way cable) fixes the header orientation and the socket crimping that keep pin 1 on pin 1, and the cable length. Rejected: IN and OUT side by side in the middle of the card (longer, crossing cables) and one multi-drop cable per bus (changes item 38, a different cable for each card count) (underside rules and keep-out: item 121)
119. Channel meter: 0805 LEDs under a printed bezel (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-09; refines items 43, 55, 76 and 86 for the channel card). The eight meter segments are 0805 LEDs placed by JLCPCB (basic parts: KENTO KT-0805G green, KT-0805Y yellow, NationStar NCD0805R1 red; no hand soldering, no fees) under a 3D-printed bezel. The bezel is opaque with one translucent window per segment, 11.6 mm tall so its face is flush with the panel's top surface (10.0 mm gap plus the 1.6 mm panel); it passes through one rectangular slot in the panel (about 5 × 49 mm) and is located by that slot and by two printed pegs in 1.5 mm non-plated holes in the card, clamped by nothing else. Segment pitch 6.0 mm: a 48 mm column, windows about 4 × 4.5 mm with 1.5 mm walls, scale labels (−30 … CLIP) printed on the panel beside it. The column's top (CLIP) is level with the fader's top travel end and runs down from there; the meter and fader scales are independent (the meter reads the pre-fader level, the fader sets gain), so their marks are not aligned. The column's position across the strip is set at the channel card layout and recorded in `MECHANICAL.md`. Rejected: round 3 mm panel holes over a hidden light guide (keeps item 86's look, but the user prefers the bezel face), square windows in the panel (routed holes), 5.0 mm pitch (labels tight) and 7.5 mm pitch spanning the full travel (tall windows light unevenly); aligning the meter 0 with the fader 0 mark (suggests a link that does not exist)
120. Panel fixing: one centred screw per end with a printed locating key (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-09; replaces the two screws per end of item 108, which otherwise stands). Each top panel end is fixed by one M3 button-head screw (ISO 7380, for a wider clamping head) on the strip's centreline into one drop-in M3 T-nut in the rail's top slot: two screws per panel. A small 3D-printed key under each panel end sits in the rail's top slot and stops the panel rotating in its own plane, keeping the strips in line with their 0.2 mm gaps. Twist from button presses near the panel edges is carried mainly by the panel and card together (the card hangs 10 mm under the panel on the pot bushings and the fader, item 107), and the panel end stays clamped flat on the rail's 20 mm top face. Check rattle and twist on the prototype; the second screw per end comes back if it is not stiff enough. Halves the screws and T-nuts (8 fewer on the prototype, 32 at full size) and speeds up removing a strip. Rejected: keeping two screws per end (proven, double the hardware) and one screw without a key (strips may drift out of line)
121. Underside of the cards and the bottom cover (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-09; settles part of `MECHANICAL.md` Open item 1; refines items 107 and 109 and backs item 112). (1) Underside parts: the chain headers at the card edges (item 109) and, elsewhere, only parts set by hand (trimmers and jumper headers, item 112), at most 10.5 mm below the PCB's bottom face (3296W trimmer 10.03 mm, jumper header with cap about 8.5 mm); everything else stays on the top side. (2) Keep-out: no underside parts other than the chain headers within 10 mm of each long edge over the header length plus 15 mm front and back (room for the IDC socket, the cable bend and fingers on the latch); provisional until the cable mock-up of item 109 confirms it; a cross band for the full-size power feed, if needed, is settled with Open item 2. (3) Bottom cover: the frame has a detachable bottom cover held by a few captive or quarter-turn screws into the rails' bottom slots, removable without touching the side cheeks, the cards or the feet; the feet therefore sit on the side cheeks or the rails, not on the cover. With the cover off, the middle of every card's underside is open to a screwdriver and fingers while the unit runs (set on its side or upside down on a soft mat). The cover's inside height clears the headers with sockets and the U-loops; that height, which sets the frame height, comes from the mock-up. Rejected: access holes in a fixed bottom plate (must line up with every card, no finger access to jumpers) and no cover at all (dust and loose parts, the cards' underside exposed)
160. Rear panel, input module position and frame shape (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-11; settles the rear part of `MECHANICAL.md` Open item 1; refines items 59, 62, 99, 108 and 121). The Rean NYS216 (decision 132) is 17.7 mm wide across its pin rows (footprint `Jack_6.35mm_Rean_NYS216_Horizontal`, drawn from the Rean drawing of 2015-05-27), so two jacks side by side need 35.4 mm, more than the 35.0 mm strip pitch (item 106). (1) Input module: the module PCB stands on edge in the front-to-back vertical plane, under the rear rail, centred on its strip; its two jacks are stacked, L/MONO above R, about 19 mm between centres, so the module is about 40 mm tall and only the jack body height plus the PCB wide across the strip. The jack nuts on the rear panel are its only fixing; the KK 254 header faces forward. (2) Rear panel: one black FR4 rear strip per channel, 34.8 mm wide like the top panels, so adding a channel adds one card, one top strip, one rear strip and one module; the master and power section get their own rear piece (Open item 2, with the power rocker cutout of item 157 and the brick jack). (3) Rear structure: the 2020 rail stays at the top and carries the top panels (item 108); the jacks sit below it; an aluminium angle (about 20 × 20 × 2 mm, cut to length like the rails) runs along the rear bottom edge. Each rear strip is fixed by an M3 screw into a T-nut in the rear rail's back slot at the top and by a screw into the angle at the bottom; the bottom cover screws to the angle at the rear (item 121; its front fixing is settled with the cover height from the mock-up). Rear inside height about 60 mm (20 mm rail plus about 40 mm of jack zone), an estimate until the module layout. (4) Frame shape: sloped. The cheeks are wedges; the front is only as tall inside as the cable mock-up needs (about 35 to 40 mm expected), the rear about 60 mm, so the top panels tilt about 4 to 5° toward the player. The bottom cover is flat. The cards hang from their panels (item 107), so the tilt changes nothing on any board; the cheeks are cut once for any channel count. (5) Input cable (item 99): 100 mm, provisional; fixed once the channel card layout places its KK header. Rejected: a narrower 6.3 mm jack so the module lies flat with the jacks side by side (rear about 45 mm, but gives up item 132 and leaves about 17.5 mm between plugs, cramped for large plugs; no part checked); a second 2020 rail at the bottom rear instead of the angle (rear about 80 mm); a rear strip held only by its top screw (an FR4 cantilever flexes about 0.8 mm under a 20 N plug insertion); a flat box about 60 mm tall all round (simpler cheeks and more air, but no playing tilt; the user chose the slope)
162. Master section, power bay, power feeds and front (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-11; settles `MECHANICAL.md` Open item 2; refines items 56, 80, 86, 109, 121, 157 and 160). The master card hangs 10 mm under its panel like a channel card (item 107), so the rails sit behind its front and rear edges and no jack on the card can reach a front or rear panel; its eleven rear jacks (main out ×2, AUX sends ×4, AUX returns ×4, SC EXT) and the headphone jack must leave the card. (1) Order and feeds: left to right, the channel cards, the master section, the power bay. Chain 1 feeds the group of up to 8 cards next to the master (where the prototype's cards sit), chain 2 the far group; each feed plugs into the power OUT header of its group's rightmost card, otherwise unused (for chain 2 the card where the 8/9 power jumper is left out, item 80). The chain 2 ribbon (about 0.5 m) runs along the rear floor in front of the input modules on printed clips on the rear bottom angle, so no cross band is needed on the cards' underside (the note in item 121); about 0.1 V drop at the sizing current (28 AWG, two conductors per rail). Channel numbers, if printed, run left to right independently of the chains. (2) Master section: 4 strips (140 mm), one top panel 139.8 mm wide (at most 360 mm long), master card at most 138 mm wide and 318 mm deep, chain headers on its left edge at the channel distance (item 109); three control columns (compressor and sidechain, AUX, master/phones/meter), set at layout. The master section was proposed as 4 or 5 strips; 4 fits with the power items in the bay. (3) Master rear jacks: one rear piece 139.8 mm wide, 12 positions in 2 rows × 6 at about 23 mm: the 11 jacks and the ground-lift switch SW901. They sit on two flat passive row boards fixed only by the jack nuts, one above the other: an output board (main L/R, AUX send 1 L/R, AUX send 2 L/R) and an input board (AUX return 1 L/R, AUX return 2 L/R, SC EXT, ground lift), each cabled to the master card by one ribbon; inputs and outputs stay on separate boards. This needs the NYS216 at most about 17 mm tall above its board (the drawing was not reachable; an unlabelled 0.61 in on a distributor listing may be its height): measure a sample; if taller, fall back to on-edge 2-jack boards like the input module (six boards, the AUX returns reusing `input-module-6p3`). A third jack row was considered: it fits but needs about 76 mm inside at the rear instead of 60. (4) Front: a single 2020 rail; the front inside height is the rail's 20 mm and the bottom cover screws into its bottom slot (item 121 as first written), so no front bottom angle or front strips. With about 60 mm at the rear the panels tilt about 6° (item 160 expected 4-5°). The cards clear: at the front only fader and part pins pass the card's underside; underside parts (10.5 mm, item 121, 22.1 mm below the panel underside) need about 40 mm behind the front rail's inner face, and the chain headers sit in the rear half (item 109). The cable mock-up now only checks that the header zone has room. Headphones (item 56): a panel-mounted 6.3 mm stereo jack held by its nut on the master top panel, its body through a cutout in the master card, wired by a short twisted cable to a header on the card (the unit's only hand-wired jack; item 118 rejected hand wiring for the CV jack on every card, not for one jack); it needs about 30 mm of depth, so it sits at least about 90 mm behind the front edge. Rejected: a front bottom angle with FR4 front strips and the jack on the front (two front members), a small jack board on spacers under the master card (one more board), a 3.5 mm headphone jack (weaker on stage). (5) Power bay: one strip at the right end with a plain top strip (34.8 mm; the power LED may move there, board session). The power board stands on edge in the front-to-back plane like a fin, hangs from the bay's top strip on two brackets and is fixed at its rear edge to the bay's rear strip, which carries the brick jack (KPJX-4S) and the rocker (item 157); outline up to about 300 × 35 mm following the slope. Only panels carry it (the user keeps the cheeks free of support duties); FR4 panels and insulating hardware keep PGND off the frame (invariant 8). The bay is a module: the user plans two power module sizes, one for up to 8 channel cards and one for larger builds, so small units do not pay for the full-size supply (item 96: full-size supply after the prototype is measured); same bay, same ribbons (`CHAIN.md`). Rejected: the board on the right cheek (the user keeps cheeks free of support duties) and on edge behind the master rear (the master section would grow). Unit width: 35 mm × (channels + 5) plus cheeks: about 315 mm for the prototype, 735 mm for 16 channels. (6) Master regulators U901-U903 stay TO-220 with heatsinks (about 19.7 mm, at most 11.5 °C/W) on the underside of the master card, hand-soldered, behind the card's middle, up to 25 mm below the PCB (a master-only exception to item 121's 10.5 mm); one heatsink per regulator, at least 3 mm from each other, the frame and the cover (live tabs: LM317 output, LM337 −20 V input). (7) Master meter: 0805 LEDs under printed bezels as item 119: 2 × 12 at 6.0 mm pitch plus the 5 gain-reduction LEDs, the channel's JLCPCB basic LEDs (KT-0805G, KT-0805Y, NCD0805R1), placed by JLCPCB; replaces the 3 mm LEDs of item 86 on the master

167. Heat budget and ventilation (`/system` 2026-10-13; settles `MECHANICAL.md` Open item 2; refines items 121, 160 and 162; keeps item 95's junction target and applies it at the new air temperature). (1) Dissipation, typical at 20 V raw (`tools/heat_budget.py`, from both cards' `power_budget.py`): channel card 4.1 W (about 1 W of it in its regulators), master card 11.7 W (LM317 1.60 W, LM337 1.25 W, L7805 0.32 W), power board loss at about 88 % conversion **[estimate]**; total inside about 32 W for the prototype (4 cards), 50 W for 8 and 87 W for 16 cards. The rises below use typical dissipation; at worst case the air runs warmer. (2) Cooling model: natural convection and radiation from the top panels (about 6 W/m²K), bottom cover (3) and the sides (5), textbook estimates; stack flow over about 50 mm from the bottom cover to the rear slots, discharge coefficient 0.6, the perforated bottom cover taken as much larger than the exhaust. A sealed box rises about 25 K (prototype) to 31 K (16 cards). The unit is low (20 to 60 mm inside), so the chimney is weak and the panels carry most of the heat: generous slots buy only 4 to 7 K. (3) Ruling: passive vents, no fan on the prototype. Intake: a perforated bottom cover (the feet on the cheeks or rails keep it off the table, item 121). Exhaust: each channel rear strip gets four routed slots 2 mm wide and about 30 mm long in the jack zone, two in each band between a jack nut and the strip edge (from the left edge at about 2.5-4.5 and 6.5-8.5 mm, mirrored at 26.3-28.3 and 30.3-32.3 mm, with 2.5 mm edge margins and 2 mm FR4 bars), about 2.4 cm² per strip; the legends and the top screw move to the 20 mm of strip in front of the rail, where no air passes; the master rear piece (about 3 cm² between its 12 positions) and the power bay rear strip (about 2 cm²) get slots where the jacks, rocker and brick jack leave room. No slots in the cheeks (the user's choice) and none in the top panels (drinks on stage). The jack nut is taken as about 15 mm across (not on the Rean drawing; measure the sample). (4) Result: exhaust about 15 cm² (prototype), 24 cm² (8 cards), 43 cm² (16 cards), giving about 20, 22 and 24 K of rise; holding 15 K would need about 52, 98 and 189 cm², which the rear cannot give without cheek slots or a raised rear (rejected: the first by the user, the second as a frame change for a few kelvin). Design point: inside air 22 K above room temperature, 57 °C on a 35 °C stage, about 47 °C in a 25 °C room; it covers builds up to 8 cards. (5) Parts at 57 °C air (datasheets read 2026-10-13 by the part-facts helper): the tightest are the commercial-grade parts at 0 to +70 °C ambient, ST TL072CDT (DocID2298 Rev 8 p.4), TI NE5532DR (SLOS075K p.3), TI TL062CDR (SLOS078N p.6), TI LM339DR (SLCS006Z p.6) and TI LM13700MX (SNOSBW2F, orderable table), leaving about 13 K for the local rise over a card; Vishay DG413DY −40 to +85 °C (doc 61564 Rev B p.2), SSI2144 and SSI2162 −40 to +85 °C (Rev 3.0 and Rev 1.2, p.2), TI OPA2171 specified −40 to +125 °C (SBOS516H p.6); onsemi LM317 and LM337 (D2PAK and TO-220) TJ 0 to +125 °C (LM317/D Rev 18 p.2 and p.10, LM337/D Rev 11 p.2 and p.8; RthJC 5 °C/W), ST L7805CV TJ 0 to +125 °C (DS0422 Rev 36 p.4), TI TPS54560 TJ −40 to +150 °C (SLVSBN0C p.5); ROQANG RVT electrolytics −55 to +105 °C, 2000 h at 105 °C (RVT datasheet p.1). (6) Master heatsinks: item 95's target (junction at most about 100 °C at worst case) stands, now at 57 °C air: at the worst-case 3.44 W the 11.5 °C/W heatsink gives about 117 °C (typical 1.60 W: about 85 °C), so the master needs about 6.5 °C/W (with 6 °C/W junction to sink) inside the 25 mm underside zone, or the user relaxes 95 in a later session. The channel cards' D²PAK regulators (about 0.5 W typical each) on copper are not affected. (7) Measurement: on the prototype, a temperature probe at the master heatsinks and over the middle channel card, one hour with every LED lit and signal on every channel; the result replaces the coefficients in `tools/heat_budget.py`. (8) Full size: about 24 K passive (59 °C on a 35 °C stage, about 11 K from the 70 °C parts); like its supply (item 96), its cooling is decided after the prototype measurement, a slow quiet fan in the power bay being the expected answer, or less dissipation. (9) Geometry to check (input module session): the rear bottom angle of item 160 (20 × 20 × 2 mm) must not cover the lower slots or the lower jack's body (about 10 mm above the floor); place it under the jack zone or shorten its vertical leg. Rejected: a sealed box (about 25-31 K, 60-66 °C on a hot stage, too close to the 70 °C parts); a 15 K target (would need cheek slots or a raised rear); a fan from the start (noise and a part that can fail on stage, not needed for the prototype). A first version of this ruling the same session set 15 K with cheek slots on an optimistic exhaust estimate; the architect review and the user's questions corrected it before commit
