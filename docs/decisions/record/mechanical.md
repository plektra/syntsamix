# Mechanical decisions

Frame, panels, strip layout and front-panel parts.

Part of the decision log; the index of all decisions is `INDEX.md`. Numbers are global and never reused. Text marked **[proposed]** still needs the user's confirmation.

## Decisions

55. Channel strip layout follows the signal flow top to bottom: trim, cutoff, resonance, filter bypass, AUX 1, AUX 2, SC send and compressor bus buttons, PFL, mute, then meter beside the fader (item 106 puts the cutoff CV jack on its own spot above CUTOFF and sets the strip envelope) (meter: printed bezel in one panel slot, item 119)
59. Mechanical format: desktop unit; one PCB plus FR4 top panel (about 35 mm) per strip; rear-panel PCB-mount jacks; aluminium rail frame cut to length with side cheeks; chain ribbons under the strips
75. Buttons: plain latching DPDT PCB switches, CW Industries GPBS850N (Electrokit 41012905, about 0.57 € each at 25+), with 3D-printed translucent caps lit from below by an 0805 LED beside each switch. Latching mechanics keep every button state through a power cut, which is a must (for example a power cut during a gig). Illuminated latching switches were rejected on cost (about 4 $ each for the cheapest suitable part, E-Switch LP4). Pressed = pins 2-3 and 5-6 closed (datasheet), so the logic line is on pin 3 and the LED on pin 6
86. Meters use small round 3 mm LEDs, on the channel cards (8 segments) and on the master (12 segments per side). Ready-made segmented bar graphs and square LED stacks were judged too bulky; the bar-graph sample is dropped from the second Electrokit order (channel meter refined by item 119: 0805 LEDs under a printed bezel)
106. Strip envelope and mechanical contract (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-08; refines items 55, 59 and 68). Strip pitch 35.0 mm, top panel strip 34.8 mm, channel card PCB at most 33.0 mm wide, top panel at most 320 mm deep (the panel column sums to about 300 to 320 mm, an estimate until the layout). The cutoff CV jack moves from beside CUTOFF to its own spot centred on the strip directly above it (about 18 mm of depth): at 35 mm a 6.3 mm plug body (12 to 15 mm across) beside the knob would leave about 15 mm for the most-played knob. Panels stay FR4 (aluminium considered and set aside for now; still a product-version option). The mechanical numbers boards must fit live in a new contract, `docs/MECHANICAL.md`, changed only by a confirmed decision. Rejected: 40 mm strips with the jack beside the knob (unit and every board and panel about 14 % larger), and 35 mm with a small (about 12 mm) CUTOFF knob beside the jack
107. Panel stack (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-08; refines item 59, adds to `MECHANICAL.md`). The top of the PCB sits 10.0 mm below the underside of the 1.6 mm FR4 panel, set by the Alpha 9 mm pots (RD901F-40 and RD902F-40: 10 mm body, M7×0.75 bushing 5 mm long, shaft L measured from the body top; Alpha drawing SLH-211-414 and 9 mm catalog page 5). The pots fix the panel with washer (0.4 mm) and nut (2 mm): 4.0 of the 5 mm thread. The fader (Bourns PTA6043, datasheet Rev. 12/14: frame top 6.5 mm above the PCB, two M2 tapped holes 71 mm apart, DP lever 15 mm above the frame) is screwed to the panel with two M2 screws through 3.5 mm spacers, so the lever stands about 10 mm above the panel and the push of a live set goes into the panel, not the solder joints. Assembly order (the user's rule): the fader is screwed to the panel first and only then soldered to the PCB. Parts on the top side under the panel are at most 9 mm tall (1 mm clearance). Rejected: tall parts on the underside (hand soldering on the bottom, in the way of the ribbons) and a taller gap on standoffs with pots not fixed to the panel (knob forces into the PCB only); a fader held only by its PCB pins (underside parts allowed by items 112 and 121)
108. Card fixing (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-08; refines items 59 and 106). Each top panel rests on the front and back 2020 rails, covering each rail's full 20 mm top face, and is fixed with two M3 screws per end (four per panel), about 24 mm apart across the strip, into drop-in M3 T-nuts in the rail's top slot (slot centre 10 mm from the rail edge). The card hangs from its panel by the pots and the fader. The 320 mm of item 106 is the control area between the rails' inner faces; the panel is then at most 360 mm long, and the PCB fits between the rails, at most 318 mm deep (1 mm clearance to each rail; at 10 mm under the panel it cannot pass over a 20 mm deep rail). Rejected: one screw per end (less stiff against twist from buttons near the edges) and standoffs to a bottom plate (a full-width plate and hardware in every slot) (screws per end replaced by item 120)
109. Chain header placement (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-08; refines items 38 and 59). The master section sits at the right end of the unit, with the power board beside it. On every channel card the audio (2×17) and power (2×4) IN headers run along the left edge and the OUT headers along the right edge, on the underside, long axis front to back, all at one distance from the front; the channel card layout chooses that distance (in the rear half, clear of the fader) and records it in `MECHANICAL.md`, and the master card's headers follow it. Neighbours are linked by identical short jumper cables in a U-loop under the gap between card n's OUT and card n+1's IN (about 12 mm apart, about 60 to 80 mm of cable). Before the first PCB order a mock-up (two box headers about 12 mm apart on scrap board, a real 34-way cable) fixes the header orientation and the socket crimping that keep pin 1 on pin 1, and the cable length. Rejected: IN and OUT side by side in the middle of the card (longer, crossing cables) and one multi-drop cable per bus (changes item 38, a different cable for each card count) (underside rules and keep-out: item 121)
119. Channel meter: 0805 LEDs under a printed bezel (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-09; refines items 43, 55, 76 and 86 for the channel card). The eight meter segments are 0805 LEDs placed by JLCPCB (basic parts: KENTO KT-0805G green, KT-0805Y yellow, NationStar NCD0805R1 red; no hand soldering, no fees) under a 3D-printed bezel. The bezel is opaque with one translucent window per segment, 11.6 mm tall so its face is flush with the panel's top surface (10.0 mm gap plus the 1.6 mm panel); it passes through one rectangular slot in the panel (about 5 × 49 mm) and is located by that slot and by two printed pegs in 1.5 mm non-plated holes in the card, clamped by nothing else. Segment pitch 6.0 mm: a 48 mm column, windows about 4 × 4.5 mm with 1.5 mm walls, scale labels (−30 … CLIP) printed on the panel beside it. The column's top (CLIP) is level with the fader's top travel end and runs down from there; the meter and fader scales are independent (the meter reads the pre-fader level, the fader sets gain), so their marks are not aligned. The column's position across the strip is set at the channel card layout and recorded in `MECHANICAL.md`. Rejected: round 3 mm panel holes over a hidden light guide (keeps item 86's look, but the user prefers the bezel face), square windows in the panel (routed holes), 5.0 mm pitch (labels tight) and 7.5 mm pitch spanning the full travel (tall windows light unevenly); aligning the meter 0 with the fader 0 mark (suggests a link that does not exist)
120. Panel fixing: one centred screw per end with a printed locating key (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-09; replaces the two screws per end of item 108, which otherwise stands). Each top panel end is fixed by one M3 button-head screw (ISO 7380, for a wider clamping head) on the strip's centreline into one drop-in M3 T-nut in the rail's top slot: two screws per panel. A small 3D-printed key under each panel end sits in the rail's top slot and stops the panel rotating in its own plane, keeping the strips in line with their 0.2 mm gaps. Twist from button presses near the panel edges is carried mainly by the panel and card together (the card hangs 10 mm under the panel on the pot bushings and the fader, item 107), and the panel end stays clamped flat on the rail's 20 mm top face. Check rattle and twist on the prototype; the second screw per end comes back if it is not stiff enough. Halves the screws and T-nuts (8 fewer on the prototype, 32 at full size) and speeds up removing a strip. Rejected: keeping two screws per end (proven, double the hardware) and one screw without a key (strips may drift out of line)
121. Underside of the cards and the bottom cover (`/system mechanical` 2026-10-09; settles part of `MECHANICAL.md` Open item 1; refines items 107 and 109 and backs item 112). (1) Underside parts: the chain headers at the card edges (item 109) and, elsewhere, only parts set by hand (trimmers and jumper headers, item 112), at most 10.5 mm below the PCB's bottom face (3296W trimmer 10.03 mm, jumper header with cap about 8.5 mm); everything else stays on the top side. (2) Keep-out: no underside parts other than the chain headers within 10 mm of each long edge over the header length plus 15 mm front and back (room for the IDC socket, the cable bend and fingers on the latch); provisional until the cable mock-up of item 109 confirms it; a cross band for the full-size power feed, if needed, is settled with Open item 2. (3) Bottom cover: the frame has a detachable bottom cover held by a few captive or quarter-turn screws into the rails' bottom slots, removable without touching the side cheeks, the cards or the feet; the feet therefore sit on the side cheeks or the rails, not on the cover. With the cover off, the middle of every card's underside is open to a screwdriver and fingers while the unit runs (set on its side or upside down on a soft mat). The cover's inside height clears the headers with sockets and the U-loops; that height, which sets the frame height, comes from the mock-up. Rejected: access holes in a fixed bottom plate (must line up with every card, no finger access to jumpers) and no cover at all (dust and loose parts, the cards' underside exposed)
