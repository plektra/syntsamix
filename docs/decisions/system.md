# System and chain decisions

System scope, signal levels, buses, cross-board functions and the chain between cards (audio and power ribbons, grounding).

Part of the decision log; the index of all decisions is `INDEX.md`. Numbers are global and never reused. Text marked **[proposed]** still needs the user's confirmation.

## Decisions

1. Pro audio mixer for connecting synthesizers and instruments in electronic music live performances
2. Modular by design
3. Prototype: 4 stereo channels
13. The compressor processes a dedicated compressor bus; each channel assigns to main or compressor bus
14. AUX sends and returns are stereo by default; the design must also support mono inputs and therefore mono AUX sends
25. Inputs accept hot Eurorack modular levels (up to ±12 V peak) as well as line levels, without an external attenuator
27. All jacks on the mixer are 6.3 mm (channel inputs: 2x 6.3 mm TRS, L/MONO and R; also AUX sends and returns, sidechain input, outputs and headphones). No 3.5 mm jacks on the prototype; Eurorack users connect with 3.5 mm to 6.3 mm cables. Replaces the earlier plans of 3.5 mm alongside 6.3 mm and of 3.5 mm only. Other jack sizes can come later through the interchangeable input module (backlog)
28. PFL is in the prototype: a button per channel (post-filter, pre-fader, pre-mute) feeding a stereo cue bus; headphones switch to cue automatically while any PFL is active; PFL-active LED. No solo-in-place
30. Channel strips can be added one at a time; each stereo channel card has no direct dependency on other channel cards
32. Budget: relatively tight; prefer cost-effective parts where the audible difference is small
34. Expansion: chainable bus from the start, so the system can grow without a fixed slot count (form chosen in item 38)
38. Chain unit: per card. Each channel card has identical IN and OUT connectors wired straight through, linked to its neighbour by short ribbon cables; the master card sits at one end. No backplane PCB
39. Practical maximum: 16 channel cards
40. Chain power: unregulated about ±20 V nominal (raised from ±18 V so the far card's regulators stay out of dropout after ripple and cable drop); each card regulates its own ±15 V. Audio ground and power ground use separate conductors
41. Separate ribbons: a 34-pin IDC audio ribbon (buses, logic lines, interleaved audio grounds, spares) and a keyed 8-pin IDC power ribbon (2× V+, 2× V−, 4× ground). The 10- and 16-pin sizes are avoided so a Eurorack power cable cannot be plugged in
42. Chain pinouts, signal definitions and grounding rules as in `CHAIN.md`. Sidechain bus is mono. Power enters the chain at the master card, which is also the only point where audio ground and power ground join (since items 80 and 81 power enters at the separate power board; the AGND-PGND join stays on the master card)
46. Confirmed in one batch: analog signal path; +4 dBu nominal, about +20 dBu internal maximum, +24 dBu only at balanced outputs; performance targets in SPEC.md section 5; DC-coupled receiver inputs with AC coupling about 3 Hz after the receiver; click-free mute via the VCA; fader bottom drives the VCA past -100 dB (fully off); sidechain LPF 40 to 500 Hz, 12 dB/octave, with bypass; channel independence rules (identical slots, master-side bus summing, wired-OR logic, no shared parts, per-card regulation); master level through an SSI2162 VCA and linear pot; headphone volume knob; balanced outputs on 6.3 mm TRS; external supply delivering unregulated about ±20 V
61. Grounding: jack sleeves to their card's AGND; FR4 panels isolate jacks from the frame; frame bonded to the star point at the master card only, with a ground-lift switch (RC when lifted). Chain connectors are 2.54 mm shrouded keyed box headers (2x17, 2x4) with 28 AWG IDC ribbon; part numbers chosen during the schematic
64. Soft clipping after each channel's input trim stage and on the master bus output, onset about 6 dB below the internal maximum (about +14 dBu). Inspired by the Intellijel Jellymix
66. Sidechain ducking through the channel VCAs (inspired by the Boredbrain Xcelon SL): the master card turns the filtered sidechain into an envelope (THRESHOLD, DEPTH, DECAY; fast fixed attack) on chain line SC_ENV (was SPARE1); each channel has a DUCK button that adds it to its VCA control; AUX returns get a DUCK switch too. Complements the compressor bus
69. Stereo button functions are switched electronically: each latching button has two contact sets, one driving a logic line into a DG413 quad analog switch (Vishay, SOIC-16; 2 NO + 2 NC, so one chip selects between two stereo signals) and one lighting its LED. Audio never runs through mechanical contacts. Each channel card makes a +5 V logic supply locally (for the DG413 VL pins). Refines decision 54
80. Power ribbon current (open since item 78) is solved by injecting power in groups of 8 cards: chain 1 feeds cards 1 to 8, chain 2 runs a separate power ribbon to card 9 and on to card 16 (no cable between cards 8 and 9). With the separate power board (item 81) both chains start at the power board, which also feeds the master card through its own ribbon, so no single ribbon carries more than 8 cards. About 0.53 A per pin at full size (restated by item 96: 0.76 A per pin under its sizing rule; 0.74 A after items 102 and 103, figures in `ARCHITECTURE.md`). Channel cards are unchanged; the 4-channel prototype uses only chain 1
96. Power sizing rule (system validation 2026-10-06, finding 2; `tools/system_power_budget.py`, which runs both cards' `power_budget.py`). A card's own parts (regulators, heatsinks, its ribbon segment) are sized for its worst case: every IC at datasheet maximum, every LED lit, full signal load. Parts shared by many cards (the DC-DC, the ribbon pins at the start of a chain) are sized for IC quiescent current at typical × 1.5 plus the use-dependent loads (LEDs, relays, signal, headphones) at their maximum, because datasheet maxima do not all occur together across many parts while the use-dependent loads can. Per rail at 20 V raw: channel card 189 / 157 mA (+15 / −15 V), master 597 / 485 mA. Prototype (4 cards and the master): 1.35 / 1.11 A, about 49 W; the prototype DC-DC gets about 2 A per rail. Full size (16 cards and the master): 3.6 / 3.0 A, about 132 W, the supply rating for 16 cards. Power ribbon: 0.76 A per pin with 8 cards per chain, so decision 80 stands provided the chosen IDC headers, sockets and cable are rated at least 1 A per contact (checked when they are chosen). The 1.5 factor is a judgment: replace it with bench measurements of the prototype cards before a full-size supply is bought. (Since item 100 "the DC-DC" means the power board's own converters, and the full-size step is sizing or revising them rather than buying a module. The figures above are as decided; after items 102 and 103 the script gives slightly lower ones, kept current in `ARCHITECTURE.md`)
97. SC_ENV scale and ducking circuit (system validation 2026-10-06, finding 1; refines items 66, 72 and 90; changes `CHAIN.md`). SC_ENV is negative: 0 V = no ducking, −1 V = 10 dB of gain reduction, DEPTH 0 to −4 V = 0 to 40 dB (decision 90's range unchanged). Each channel card and each master AUX return feeds the DUCK-switched SC_ENV as a current into the control summer's virtual earth (VSUM) through 30k1 against the 10k feedback resistor (0.332 V/V, 10.06 dB/V at the SSI2162's −33 mV/dB), and VPLUS goes to AGND, so the ducking no longer depends on the fader position or the superdiode states (the old non-inverting input gave 4.6 to 10 dB/V). The master drives SC_ENV with a unity inverter in place of the follower (drawn instead as a negative DEPTH voltage and the existing follower, item 104), its feedback taken after the 100 Ω series resistor so the line stays low impedance with up to 18 loads of 30k1 (about 1.7 kΩ, 2.4 mA at −4 V); otherwise the depth would change with the number of ducking cards. A Schottky diode from SC_ENV to AGND on the master limits a positive fault excursion to about +0.3 V (at most about 3 dB of boost); a disconnected line gives no ducking. Ducking passes through the channel's 10 ms control smoothing (R215, C224), so the effective attack is about 10 ms instead of the master's 3 ms; accepted, to be heard on the breadboard. Positive SC_ENV through an inverter on every card was rejected (18 extra stages); a documented fader-dependent scale was rejected (channels would duck differently)
98. Logic returns to PGND (system validation 2026-10-06, finding 4; refines item 42, changes `CHAIN.md`): the open-collector PFL_ACT drivers (channel Q301, master Q501) have their emitters on PGND, and every button pull-down (100 kΩ from a button control line) goes to PGND instead of AGND, on both cards. The PFL LED and pull-up current (about 2.5 mA) then flows out on the PFL_ACT conductor and back through the power ribbons, never in AGND, so invariant 4 holds without exceptions. The DG41x GND pins stay on AGND; the millivolts between PGND and AGND do not matter at 5 V logic levels. No parts added. Documenting the exception instead was rejected
101. Floating supply (`/system power conversion` 2026-10-08; follows item 100, refines items 42 and 61, changes `CHAIN.md` and adds invariant 8). Whatever feeds the power ribbons must have a DC output that floats from mains earth: today the Class II 24 V brick of item 100; a future external linear supply box, an internal mains supply or the full-size supply must keep it. PGND is earthed nowhere; the only ground reference inside the mixer is the star point on the master card, and the mixer takes its earth reference from connected gear (as most desktop audio gear with an external Class II adapter does). The ground-lift switch (item 61) stays as it is: the frame connects only to the star point, directly or through the RC when lifted, which matters when the frame touches something earthed (for example rack ears). The prototype keeps the Class II brick; an internal mains supply (Class I: safety earth hard-wired to the chassis, the lift moving between signal ground and chassis) and an external linear supply box are backlog items. Rejected: an isolated converter stage on the power board (the cost item 100 removed) and a rule recorded only as a power-board part requirement (lost at the next supply change)

## Rationale notes

- Why PFL in the prototype: without it, trimming a channel by ear happens in the main mix where the audience hears it. Tapping before the VCA makes PFL pre-mute, so a channel can be prepared while muted. Solo-in-place was rejected because an accidental press silences the main mix.
- Why separate power and audio ribbons: with 16 cards, the card nearest the supply carries every card's current (about 3.6 A per rail at full size, decision 96). A separate power cable keeps that current, the rectifier ripple and switching spikes away from the sensitive mix bus lines, and can be heavier or fed from both ends. Separate ground conductors matter most: shared ground with power return current would put hum on the buses.
- Why no submix per block of cards: the noise is dominated by the sum of the channels' own noise, which grouping cannot change; a local submix lowers the total by only about 0.3 dB at 16 channels (estimate) and would break per-card independence.
- Why keep PFL with channel meters: the meter shows level, PFL lets the performer hear a channel (patch, tuning, filter, hum, timing) before the audience does. The channel meters take over trimming, so the master meter no longer needs to switch to cue.
- Why a compressor bus: classic pumping keys the music from the kick. On a master-bus compressor the kick would duck itself; a separate bus lets the kick stay on main and trigger the compressor.
